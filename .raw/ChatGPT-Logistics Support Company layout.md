# Logistics Support Company (LSC) – Doctrinal Layout

### **1. Mission**
To provide second-line (and in some cases limited first-line) combat service support to a parent battalion, regiment, or task force. The LSC ensures continuous sustainment across supply, transport, maintenance, and medical functions to enable freedom of maneuver and endurance in operations.

---

### **2. Command & Control**
- **Company Headquarters (Coy HQ)**
  - Company Commander (Major / Captain)  
  - Company 2IC (Captain / Lieutenant)  
  - Company Sergeant Major (WO2)  
  - Operations / Liaison Cell  
  - Administration Cell  

The Coy HQ plans, coordinates, and synchronizes all logistics support activities and liaises with higher echelon logistics (e.g., Brigade Support Battalion / Combat Service Support Battalion).

---

### **3. Sub-Units / Platoons**
The LSC is typically broken into functional platoons:

#### a. **Transport Platoon**
- Role: Provides lift for personnel, ammunition, supplies, and water.  
- Composition:  
  - Platoon HQ  
  - Medium/Heavy Transport Sections (general cargo, POL, water tankers)  
  - Recovery/Light transport  

#### b. **Supply Platoon**
- Role: Manages receipt, storage, and distribution of supplies (Classes I–V, VIII, IX as applicable).  
- Composition:  
  - Platoon HQ  
  - Supply Section (rations, fuel, water)  
  - Ammunition Section  
  - POL (Petrol, Oil, Lubricants) Section  

#### c. **Maintenance Platoon**
- Role: Provides field-level (2nd line) maintenance support for vehicles, weapons, and equipment.  
- Composition:  
  - Platoon HQ  
  - Vehicle Maintenance Section (fitters, recovery mechanics)  
  - Weapons/Equipment Repair Section  
  - Electrical/Communications Repair Section  

#### d. **Medical Platoon (Role 1+)**
- Role: Provides medical treatment and evacuation (beyond unit aid posts).  
- Composition:  
  - Platoon HQ  
  - Treatment Section (medical officers, medics, nursing staff)  
  - Evacuation Section (ambulance assets)  
  - Medical Supply Section  

---

### **4. Key Capabilities**
- **Supply Chain:** Push and pull of rations, fuel, water, and ammunition.  
- **Maintenance:** Light to medium-level repairs, battle damage assessment and repair (BDAR).  
- **Transport:** Convoy operations, resupply, and troop lift.  
- **Medical Support:** Triage, stabilization, and casualty evacuation to higher Role facilities.  
- **Flexibility:** Able to tailor logistics packages (LOGPACs) for different mission sets.  

---

### **5. Typical Strength**
- ~150–200 personnel (varies by doctrine and task force design).  
- Mix of officers, senior NCOs, specialist trades (mechanics, drivers, medics, suppliers).  

---

### **6. Doctrinal Employment**
- Normally co-located with the parent battalion’s echelon or Combat Service Support Area (CSSA).  
- Operates on “push” and “pull” logistics principles.  
- Interfaces with:
  - **Supported combat units** (frontline battalions/companies).  
  - **Higher echelon support** (Brigade Support Battalion / Theatre Logistics).  

---

⚖️ **Variants / Notes**  
- Some armies combine **Supply & Transport** into one **Distribution Platoon**.  
- Some split maintenance into **Vehicle & Armament Platoons**.  
- Medical support can be organic (Role 1+) or attached from a brigade medical company.  
- UAV/digital logistics tracking is increasingly doctrinal in modern forces.
