Deep-dive analysis prepared ahead of a Monday meeting (week of 2026-08-11) on PG innovation governance — chaired by J3, with CDD (J8), an AI-lead Colonel, Falcons Brigade (MAKHLAB 1), and the former S&T Center (now absorbed into CDD). ## The Core Diagnosis

No single missing piece — five partially-built structures, none formally established, with overlapping and in places contradictory claims to the same territory:

| Structure | What it actually is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **TEIW** (SBTDWS) | Tier-1 execution only — bounded, 30–90 day experiments, existing resources, no R&D, no procurement | Drafted, interim governance, not endorsed |
| **ATI-Hub** | Proposed "build" engine — RDW/TEX-Wing/UxS-FPV Wing, DLOD/FIC ownership, 0–100 unit production | **Pre-dates Paper 2. Not endorsed within Paper 2 or any subsequent PG 2030 planning document.** Treat as an SBTDWS-originated proposal, not an existing or semi-existing structure. |
| **PGIC / "Innovation Command"** | Enterprise portfolio governance — priorities, resourcing, transition gates | Tasked on paper (Aug 2026 task register, 9 numbered tasks) but not established — no OF-5, no manning, no decree |
| **J3 WDC** | Owns FDC 5 mandate, kill/continue authority for experiments | Governance concept; D-11 (its own relationship to SBTDWS) unformalised |
| **WG5 (Falcons Brigade)** | Cross-unit coordination of the MAKHLAB *family specifically* | Proposed. Not a general innovation-governance mandate — see note below |
| **CDD / J8** | The PG's actual, standing capability-development authority — does all PG R&D/CAPDEV today, always has, absorbed the former S&T Center | Real, active, unnamed in any PG 2030 document until 2026-08-08 |

> [!contradiction] ATI-Hub's own governance framing conflicts with PGIC's — and ATI-Hub is the unendorsed one
> [[ATI-Hub Concept]] frames "Innovation Command" as SBTDWS-owned, reporting to the DWS Commander. [[PGIC Concept]] frames the same name as enterprise-level, under PGC. **ATI-Hub pre-dates Paper 2 and was never endorsed in it** — confirmed by the user 2026-08-08. Do not present ATI-Hub as an established or semi-established governance layer at Monday's meeting; it is an SBTDWS proposal that could be *offered* as a contribution (see "What SBTDWS Can Actually Offer" below), not a structure anyone else is obligated to recognise.

> [!key-insight] Falcons Brigade is at the table because they're furthest along, not because of a governance claim
> Confirmed by the user 2026-08-08: Falcons Brigade is attending Monday's meeting specifically because they are the only unit that has actually started its MAKHLAB project (MAKHLAB 1). Every other unit has its own MAKHLAB responsibility (2/3/4/8, see [[Programme MAKHLAB]]) and innovation is meant to happen across all of them, not centre on Falcons Brigade. Their WG5 coordination lead (see [[PG 2030 Working Groups]]) is a cross-unit *MAKHLAB-family* coordination mandate, not a general innovation-governance mandate — don't let the two get conflated in the room.

## The Framework — Individual/Unit Idea to Institutionalised Capability

Built without assigning an owner.

```
1. IDEA SOURCE (individual / unit level)
   Any operator with a problem, across all units — not just Falcons Bde
   ↓
2. INTAKE / SPONSORSHIP  ← NO MECHANISM EXISTS TODAY, REGARDLESS OF WHO GOVERNS
   Only designed version anywhere in PG planning: ATI-Hub's twice-yearly
   problem-statement cycle (Problem Sponsor = unit commander, User Champion =
   SNCO/junior officer) — but ATI-Hub is unendorsed, so nothing runs today
   ↓
3. TIER 1 — GOVERNED EXPERIMENT (bounded, existing resources, 30–90 days)
   TEIW's model already exists for this: hypothesis + MOE + 3 entry criteria.
   Could be offered as the PG-wide template, not just an SBTDWS-internal tool
   ↓
4. TIER 2 GATE — needs capital / scale decision
   ← THIS is where "who leads" actually matters. Tier 1 doesn't need it.
   ↓
5. SCALE / FIELD
   DLOD/FIC-style fielding dossier, organic production at small scale,
   HOTO to a prime or standard procurement — CDD's existing lane
   ↓
6. CROSS-UNIT STANDARDISATION
   WG5 (Falcons Brigade) — once more than one unit runs the same capability family
   ↓
7. INSTITUTIONALISATION
   Doctrine (SAT Cell), training pipeline, FDC review cycle
```

**The load-bearing point for Monday: stage 2 (intake) is the actual missing piece, not stage 4 (who chairs governance).** Everyone will likely argue about who sits at the top while the bottom of the pyramid has no funnel at all.

## Reframe "Who Leads" — It's Three Questions, Not One

Maps onto Army PIT's own "big three" selection panel (see [[sources/army-pathway-for-innovation]]) and the Three-Horizon page's Innovation/R&D/Trials/CAPDEV distinction (see [[Three-Horizon Innovation Model]]). Collapsing all three into one "who owns it" question is the design mistake the Valley-of-Death research names as the root cause of dead capabilities.

1. **Portfolio governance** — priorities, resourcing, Tier-2 kill/continue, cross-unit deconfliction. This is the actual "who leads" question.
2. **Execution** — running the sprints. Should stay distributed at unit/school level (TEIW is the working template). Doesn't need centralising.
3. **Sponsorship / demand signal** — naming problems, providing operators, validating outputs. Inherently unit-level. Not a headquarters function either.

If Monday collapses all three into "does CDD or J3 own innovation," whoever wins ends up owning things they shouldn't, and the answer will be fragile.

## Red Team — What Probably Hasn't Been Considered

**ACT — raise at the table Monday:**
- **Per Frank: everyone will want to own the idea, no one will want to own the work — and whoever does grab a piece is likely to run it stovepiped.** See Feedback From Frank below. Arguably the single most important thing to say out loud before any COA discussion starts.
- Open with role clarification, not COA options — if a senior, engaged reviewer doesn't know what CDD, STC, or Col Saood actually do, the room likely doesn't share clarity either. See Feedback From Frank below.
- Whoever chairs the meeting (J3) risks becoming the default portfolio owner by accident, not design.
- Stage 2 (intake) has no owner and no mechanism today, regardless of who wins the governance question.
- WG5's MAKHLAB-coordination mandate (Falcons Brigade) is not a general innovation-governance precedent — don't let it get treated as one.
- SBTDWS's real, calibrated offer is the TEIW/EXCON/evaluation execution lane specifically (see below) — not a claim on governance.
- **The central message to land: whatever this becomes, it must be functionally and culturally separate from CDD's current day-to-day CAPDEV business** (purchasing equipment, fielding it across the organisation) — even if CDD ends up hosting or chairing it. This needs to be said explicitly, not assumed as understood by the room.

**SHAPE — frame for the room, not a Monday decision:**
- CDD is not an untested candidate — it is the PG's actual, standing capability-development authority and has run every SBTDWS CAPDEV project to date. The open risk is not "can CDD do this," it's "will CDD's existing CAPDEV/procurement culture and process absorb or dilute an innovation function bolted onto it" (see [[Valley of Death Defense Innovation]] on sequential requirements/acquisition/budget gating as the primary cause of capability death).
- CDD's absorbed former S&T Center function (long-horizon technology scanning) is the closest existing PG analog to Army PIT's FUZE — a sharper, more specific basis for a CDD-hosted option than generic "CDD leads."
- PG HQ is being asked to authorise two new-organisation-shaped decisions in the same season — SBTDWS's own MDTC transition ([[SBTDWS Core Focus — Decision Brief (Two COAs, Revised)]]) and a potential Innovation Command charter. Worth naming the bandwidth risk.
- No budget/bridge-funding mechanism exists anywhere in PG's documents (Atlantic Council's historical baseline: capabilities idle 2–4 years post-demonstration; Army PIT's answer was a 90-day target with parallel-tracked requirements/acquisition/budget).

**ESCALATE — beyond Monday:**
- Is Monday actually deciding "who leads" fresh, or is it deciding how to implement/resource an Innovation Command PG HQ already tasked on paper in the Aug 2026 register but never stood up? That framing question changes what Monday is for.
- Should PG fix working-group governance/roles as its own separate problem before this framework can be executed well, given PG's demonstrated weak WG track record (Frank)?
- ~~Who coordinates the cross-work between whatever emerges from Monday and WG5~~ — **resolved 2026-08-08:** [[Frank]], lead advisor to J3, coordinates across all PG 2030 working groups, including WG5. Each WG's lead reports back to him. He asked this question in review feedback without apparently having connected it to his own coordination role — worth a light confirmation, not a structural gap.

## COA Options for the Portfolio-Governance Question

**COA A — CDD leads**
CDD already does this work today — every SBTDWS CAPDEV project routes through them, and the absorbed S&T mission gives them an existing horizon-scanning capability. No new decree, fastest start. **Risk, and the point to land hardest in the room: CDD's core business is CAPDEV/procurement/fielding-rollout — a different culture and process from innovation scouting.** If innovation simply becomes "another CDD function" it inherits CDD's process-first operating mode, the documented primary cause of the Valley of Death. **Second risk, per Frank's independent read: CDD may currently be institutionally "weak"** — doing the work without the authority/weight to lead a governance function — meaning the mitigation below may need an explicit authority uplift, not just organisational ring-fencing. Mitigation: ring-fence a small, flat, TF59-style cell inside CDD — built from the former S&T function specifically — with delegated Tier-1/scouting authority and real institutional standing, explicitly separated from CDD's CAPDEV/procurement chain, not folded into it.

**COA B — J3 leads**
Arguably already the de facto owner on paper — owns FDC 5 governance, chairs all 8 WGs, tasked with WDC oversight. Fewest new seams. Risk: concentration — J3 already carries readiness, ORC, targeting, C2 design; venture-style scouting is a different discipline and battle rhythm from staff planning. Mitigation: J3 keeps mandate-setting authority (consistent with current FDC 5 role) but delegates day-to-day portfolio management to a distinct small cell, not the full J3 staff process.

**COA C — New entity leads (PGIC/Innovation Command, as tasked on paper but not stood up)**
Matches the international pattern (Army PIT's FUZE as a separate engine) and PG HQ's own stated direction on paper. Risk: slowest — needs decree, OF-5, manning, facility, and competes for command bandwidth against D-12 in the same window. Mitigation: stand up an interim governance board now while the formal charter runs in parallel — the same pattern TEIW/ATI-Hub's own charters already use.

**Synthesis option — Army PIT "big three" model, adapted**
A standing Innovation Governance Board rather than a single department: J3 or an interim Innovation Command lead chairs; CDD (via its former-S&T function specifically, kept distinct from its CAPDEV/procurement arm) provides scouting/capability-development rigor at the Tier-2 gate; unit reps (SBTDWS, Falcons Brigade, and — per the framework's own logic — every unit with a MAKHLAB responsibility, not just Falcons Bde) provide demand signal and run execution. Best-evidenced option given the research base.

## Feedback From Frank (2026-08-08)

Reviewer feedback on the one-page report, provided ahead of Monday. Sharpens several existing points and adds real ones.

> [!key-insight] "Everyone will want to own the idea, but no one will want to own the work"
> Frank's diagnosis, verbatim in substance: expect contest over who gets credit for the innovation *concept*, and avoidance of the unglamorous *execution/coordination* work. Worse — whoever does grab a piece of execution is likely to run it unilaterally/stovepiped, without allowing cross-input from the others. This sharpens the existing red-team point about the meeting's chair becoming a default owner — it's not just an accidental-ownership risk, it's a predicted, deliberate one. **This may be the single most important thing to say out loud on Monday**, ahead of any COA discussion.

**Role confusion is real, not hypothetical — evidence from a senior, informed reviewer:**
- **CDD (J8):** Frank's independent assessment is "pretty weak" — a different observation from the user's own framing (CDD does all PG R&D/CAPDEV today, and always has). Both can be true at once: CDD may be doing the *work* without the institutional *authority/weight* to lead a governance function. This matters directly for COA A — ring-fencing a cell inside CDD (the mitigation already proposed) may not be sufficient if CDD itself lacks the standing to make it stick; COA A may need an explicit authority/mandate uplift, not just organisational ring-fencing.
- **STC (former S&T Center, now inside CDD):** Frank has "no clue what they really do." If a senior reviewer doesn't know, this isn't a documentation gap SBTDWS can quietly fix — it's a live confusion the room itself likely shares.
- **Col Saood** — named for the first time: the AI-lead Colonel attending Monday, "apparently 'Mr AI'" per Frank, but what he actually does is unclear even to Frank.
- **Falcons Brigade:** Frank doesn't have the "furthest along on MAKHLAB 1" context already captured on [[Falcons Brigade]] — worth confirming this lands clearly in the room, since even a well-briefed reviewer didn't have it.

> [!key-insight] Recommendation: role clarification should be Monday's literal first agenda item
> If a senior, engaged reviewer doesn't know what CDD, STC, or Col Saood actually do, the room itself is unlikely to have shared clarity either. Opening with "who currently does what" — not as a formality, but as substantive agenda time — may do more to de-risk the meeting than any of the COA options below.

**New disagreement — organic manufacturing vs. UAE industry:** Frank explicitly disagrees with PG-internal manufacturing capability (the ATI-Hub manufacturing-facility idea flagged on [[ATI-Hub Concept]] and [[TEIW Standing Charter]] as "an idea we've had," not a decision). His view: UAE industry should build this, since the UAE's government-industry relationship uniquely allows PG to direct commercial/national industry to produce exactly what's needed, without PG needing to own production capability itself. This is a real, credible counter-position from someone who's seen the draft — treat the manufacturing idea as more contested than the earlier "just an idea to raise" framing suggested, and be ready for pushback specifically on this point.

**Sequencing question — governance-of-the-WG before content-of-the-WG?** Frank's assessment: this framework is valuable but might be step one *or* step two — PG may need to settle the working-group governance and roles question first, separate from the innovation-content framework itself, "which these guys don't do well anyway" (Frank's words on PG's general WG execution track record). Worth raising directly: is Monday the right forum to also fix *how working groups run*, or does that need its own separate fix first?

**Cross-WG coordination — resolved, with a wrinkle:** Frank thinks linking this effort to the MAKHLAB WG ([[PG 2030 Working Groups]] WG5, led by Falcons Brigade) is the right direction, and asked directly who coordinates the cross-work between the two. Per the user, confirmed the same day: [[Frank]] himself is the standing cross-WG coordination point for the entire PG 2030 programme — he presides over all working groups, receiving reports back from each WG's lead. He asked a question he is functionally the answer to, which may be worth a light, tactful confirmation rather than treating it as an open structural gap.
