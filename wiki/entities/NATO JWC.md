# NATO Joint Warfare Centre (JWC)

NATO's premier joint warfare training and scenario development centre, located in Stavanger, Norway. Established 2003, Full Operational Capability 2006. Subordinate to Allied Commander Transformation (ACT), Norfolk, Virginia.

Navigation: [[Research: Global ADWC Equivalents]] | [[Combat Training Centre Model]]

---

## Mission

Dual mandate: exercises and warfare development. JWC is NATO's training focal point for full-spectrum joint operational- and strategic-level warfare. It supports both SACT (transformation) and SACEUR (warfighting readiness).

**Critical distinction:** JWC operates at operational and strategic level — not tactical. It trains HQs and staffs, not brigades and below. This is a fundamentally different product from NTC/JRTC, which train tactical units.

---

## Structure

**Workforce:** NATO International Civilians + military posts from 18 NATO member nations (Canada, Czech Republic, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Italy, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Spain, Türkiye, UK, USA).

**Core Teams:**
- Scenario and scripting teams — create complex, fictitious but realistic scenarios
- Exercise control and advisory teams (EXCON)
- Higher control and Grey Cell — replicates non-military partners, adversary decision-making
- Computer simulation teams
- OPFOR (opposing forces)
- Media teams
- Communications/IT teams

---

## Exercises

Three of four core NATO multi-domain exercises:
- **STEADFAST DETERRENCE** — NATO's deterrence posture exercise
- **STEADFAST DUEL** — command and control
- **STEADFAST DAGGER** — Allied Reaction Force capstone exercise

**Scope:** Divisional to multi-corps level. Five military domains — air, land, maritime, cyberspace, and space. Exercises operate in both Article 5 (collective defence) and non-Article 5 (crisis response) modes.

---

## Scenario Development

JWC's "360-degree multi-domain setting" is its distinctive capability. Scenarios are purpose-built for each exercise, integrating all five domains into a coherent synthetic environment. The Grey Cell replicates adversary decision-making and non-military partners — a level of strategic realism that tactical CTCs don't attempt.

---

## What ADWC Can Learn

1. **Scenario design as a professional function.** JWC has dedicated scenario and scripting teams. Scenario design at ADWC (especially for Mission Rehearsal) should be treated as a specialist role, not a staff task.
2. **Grey Cell for hybrid threat replication.** A small Grey Cell controlling adversary responses, media, and non-military actors adds strategic realism to exercises even at the tactical level.
3. **Warfare development alongside exercise delivery.** JWC's dual mandate (exercises + doctrine development) directly mirrors the ADWC CTVMR + TEIW design. Lessons from exercises feed doctrine — this is the loop.
4. **Domain integration in scenario design.** JWC scripts all five domains into every exercise from the outset. ADWC's exercises should do the same even when some domains must be replicated notionally.

---

## Where ADWC Differs

JWC operates at operational/strategic level. ADWC operates at tactical/operational level. ADWC's CTVMR Wing is closer to NTC/JRTC than to JWC. However, ADWC's Mission Rehearsal function — particularly when rehearsing a full formation operation — starts to approach JWC's operational-level framing.
