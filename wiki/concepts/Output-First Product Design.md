# Output-First Product Design

A design methodology that starts with the artifact the user needs to produce — the output — and builds the workflow backwards from it. The opposite of feature-first design, which adds stages and sections until the process is comprehensive.

Navigation: [[Research: Defense Innovation Governance Tools]] | [[Capability Gap Experimentation Platform]]

---

## The Principle

Most governance tools are designed process-first: "here are all the stages, fill them in." Output-first asks: "what document does the user need to hand to their commander at the end?" and designs the minimum data collection required to produce that document reliably.

Evidence base:
- CTMS design philosophy: "operational efficiency over feature density" — governance rigor must never compromise usability for frontline coordinators (Source: [[ctms-design-patterns]])
- Army PIT principle: "speed over perfection" — the output in the hands of a soldier is more valuable than a comprehensive process (Source: [[army-pathway-for-innovation]])
- Atlantic Council: replace "tens of thousands of pages" with "six-page program overviews" — the output matters, not the documentation volume (Source: [[atlantic-council-defense-innovation-adoption-2023]])

---

## Applied to TACTIX

The output TACTIX needs to produce: a **command decision brief** — a structured one-page (or short-form) document that says:
- Here is the capability gap (defined, classified by DLOD)
- Here is what we tested (hypothesis, conditions, scenario)
- Here is what we found (observations, metrics, result category)
- Here is the recommendation (Build/Buy/Scale/Terminate with rationale)

Every section in TACTIX should exist only if it contributes data required by the command decision brief. Sections that generate interesting information but don't feed the brief are Phase 2 or cut entirely.

---

## Design Test

For each section/stage of TACTIX, ask: "If I removed this section entirely, would the command decision brief be missing something essential?"
- Yes: keep the section
- No: remove it or make it optional

Applying this test to the current 16 sections is the first step in the redesign.
