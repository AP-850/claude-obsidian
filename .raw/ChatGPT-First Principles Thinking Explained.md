**First principles thinking** is a way of solving problems by breaking things down to their most basic truths—things you *know* are true—and then building up your solution from there.

Instead of relying on assumptions, traditions, or copying others, you ask:

1. **What do I know for sure about this?** (the basic facts—“first principles”)  
2. **Why is it done this way? Can it be done differently or better?**

### Simple Example:
Instead of saying,  
*"I want a faster horse,"*  
first principles thinking would say:  
- What is the real problem? **“I want to move faster from A to B.”**  
- What are the basic facts? **“A horse is just one option. The goal is speed and distance.”**  
- Could there be a better way? **“Maybe a car, a train, or something new entirely.”**

It’s how people like **Elon Musk** think—question everything, go back to the basics, and rebuild from the ground up.

### **1. Business Example – Starting a Coffee Shop**

**Common thinking:**  
"Other coffee shops charge $5 per cup, so I should too."

**First principles thinking:**  
- What do I really need to sell a great cup of coffee?  
  → High-quality beans, water, energy, skilled staff.  
- What does each of these cost?  
  → Maybe $1.50 total.  
- Why are others charging $5?  
  → Rent, brand, marketing, inefficiency.  
- Can I find a cheaper or digital model (e.g., coffee carts, pre-order app)?

**Result:** You might create a mobile-only coffee service with lower prices and higher margins—something different and better.

---

### **2. Technology Example – Elon Musk and Rockets**

**Common thinking:**  
"Rockets are expensive because they are single-use and built by legacy companies."

**First principles thinking:**  
- What is a rocket made of?  
  → Aluminum, carbon fiber, fuel—basic materials.  
- What do these materials actually cost?  
  → Much less than the full price of the rocket.  
- So why are rockets so expensive?  
  → Outdated design, no reuse, inefficient manufacturing.  
- Can we design reusable rockets like commercial airplanes?

**Result:** SpaceX builds reusable rockets at a fraction of the cost.

---

### **3. Military Example – Infantry Loadouts in Desert Warfare**

**Common thinking:**  
"Soldiers carry 40 kg of gear because that's what doctrine says."

**First principles thinking:**  
- What does a soldier really need in the desert?  
  → Water, comms, weapon, mobility, and protection from heat.  
- Why 40 kg? What is each item for?  
  → Some items are outdated, duplicated, or not useful in desert ops.  
- What if we used drones for ammo resupply and lighter tech for comms?

**Result:** Redesign kit for mission-specific needs, reducing weight to 25–30 kg, improving endurance and mobility.

Here’s a **simple and teachable framework** for applying **First Principles Thinking**—especially useful for military, business, or innovation teams:

---

### **First Principles Thinking Framework (4 Steps)**

#### **1. Clarify the Problem**
Ask:  
- *What exactly are we trying to solve or improve?*  
- *What outcome are we aiming for?*

**Example:**  
“We want soldiers to move faster and operate longer in desert conditions.”

---

#### **2. Break It Down to First Principles**
Strip away assumptions, tradition, and “the way it’s always been done.”  
Ask:  
- *What do we know is absolutely true?*  
- *What are the fundamental components or facts?*

**Example:**  
- “A human can carry ~30% of body weight before performance drops.”  
- “Water loss and heat are bigger threats than enemy fire in the desert.”  
- “Much of the gear is not used 80% of the time.”

---

#### **3. Rebuild from the Ground Up**
Use the core truths to build a solution from scratch—ignore existing models.

Ask:  
- *If we started from zero, how would we design this?*  
- *What tools or methods could better achieve the goal?*

**Example:**  
- “Let’s use autonomous drones to resupply instead of carrying all ammo.”
- “Use modular body armor that can be stripped down based on mission duration and terrain.”

---

#### **4. Test and Iterate**
Build a small-scale test or prototype to try the new approach.  
Ask:  
- *What worked? What needs adjustment?*

**Example:**  
- Trial a reduced-kit patrol with a drone tailing for resupply.
- Gather data on fatigue, heat stress, and reaction time.

---

### **Quick Prompt Sheet for Teams (Printable Format)**

**Use during planning sessions or wargames:**

1. What’s the core problem?
2. What do we *know* is true (no assumptions)?
3. What would we do if we were inventing the solution from scratch?
4. What’s one way we can test this idea next week?
