# OC/T Cadre Development Plan

The Observer Trainer (OT) cadre is the single most important near-term investment in the ADWC transformation. It determines whether CTVMR Wing delivers learning or just delivers activity. Without it, ADWC certifies participation, not readiness.

**IOC target: Q2 2027** — 13 months from now (Q2 2026 start).

Navigation: [[sbtdws/_index]] | [[ADWC Three Wing Structure]] | [[SBTDWS 2030 Timeline]]

---

## What an OT Is — and Is Not

**Is:** A trained, accredited specialist who observes a collective serial, controls engagements against doctrine, coaches unit leaders in real time, and conducts rigorous structured AARs. Carries formal authority to issue an adverse assessment with command consequence.

**Is not:** An observer who watches and takes notes. Not a mentor who advises without authority. Not a general staff officer allocated temporarily to "run the AAR."

The authority to fail is what makes the OT cadre real. Without it, the CTVMR Wing is a sophisticated training event, not a validation function.

---

## The Pool — Who Are the OTs?

**Primary source: E&E Wing** — The Exercise and Evaluation Wing already does individual-level assessment (FST). The FST evaluator role is the closest existing competency in SBTDWS. These personnel understand observation methodology, evaluation criteria, and structured feedback in principle. They need to be retrained from squad-level FST assessment to collective-level ADW validation.

**Secondary source: Selected CTW staff** — Experienced Collective Training Wing officers and SNCOs who have run or observed company exercises. They bring collective training credibility that the E&E Wing alone may lack.

**Target pool size:** 15–20 candidates selected for the IOC pipeline.

**Selection criteria:**
- Minimum rank: Officer or SNCO with company-level exercise experience
- Operational credibility with peers and subordinates
- Communication and facilitation aptitude (can run an AAR that generates learning, not blame)
- Willing to hold and defend an adverse assessment under command pressure
- Language: English and Arabic dual capability preferred (bilingual serials)

## The Accreditation Standard

OTs are assessed against five competency domains before accreditation:

| Domain | What it covers | Assessment method |
|--------|---------------|------------------|
| 1. Standards knowledge | SAG 9, SAG 10, and ADW competency profiles — the doctrine OTs assess against | Written assessment + oral board |
| 2. Observation methodology | Structured observation without interference; event capture; evidence-based recording | Supervised practical |
| 3. AAR facilitation | Structured, non-punitive, multi-echelon AAR delivery; drawing out unit self-assessment before providing judgment | Assessed AAR delivery |
| 4. Exercise control | Safety authority, engagement control, EXROE, inject management | Practical exercise |
| 5. Reporting and escalation | Adverse assessment format; reporting chain; disagreement arbitration with PGI; command briefing | Written report + brief |

**Accreditation levels:**

| Level | Scope | Notes |
|-------|-------|-------|
| OT Level 1 (Probationary) | Platoon-level observation; co-located supervisor required | Achievable after Phase 2 supervised practice |
| OT Level 2 (Qualified) | Company-level independent; authority to issue adverse assessment at company level | Formal accreditation board; IOC gate |
| OT Level 3 (Senior) | Formation-level; can supervise Level 1 OTs; contributes to accreditation boards | FOC gate; experienced cadre only |

**IOC requires:** Minimum 8 OTs at Level 2 or above — sufficient to cover one company-level validation serial with full echelon coverage.

## Training Pipeline — Four Phases

### Phase 0 — Foundation and Selection (Month 1–2)
*Now — Q3 2026*

**Actions:**
- [ ] Publish OT selection criteria (Commandant directive)
- [ ] Issue call for nominations from E&E Wing and CTW
- [ ] Conduct selection board: assess operational credibility, communication, and temperament
- [ ] Confirm 15–20 candidates
- [ ] **Trigger SAT Cell dependency:** ADW competency profiles must be at draft stage before Phase 1 begins — OTs cannot learn to assess against standards that don't exist yet. SAT Cell to publish ADW competency profiles v0.1 by end of Phase 0.

**Output:** Confirmed OT candidate list; ADW competency profiles v0.1 published.

---

### Phase 1 — Foundation Training (Month 2–6)
*Q3 2026 — Q4 2026*

Structured academic and theoretical programme. Mix of classroom, directed study, and short attachments.

**Block 1 — ADW Standards and Doctrine (4 weeks)**
- SAG 9 (Combined UW) standards — deep study; what is being validated
- SAG 10 (R&CB) standards — secondary standard
- ADW competency profiles (SAT Cell output) — the competency framework OTs assess against
- ADWC validation framework v0.1 (from ADWC design) — how the system works
- Assessment: written examination on standards knowledge

**Block 2 — Observation Methodology (3 weeks)**
- Structured observation techniques: SALUTE, event capture, evidence recording
- Observation without interference — when to intervene vs when to record
- Multi-echelon observation (platoon, company, battle group simultaneously)
- Common OT errors: over-coaching, premature intervention, confirmation bias
- Practical: observe a CTW exercise in observer role only (no evaluation authority yet)

**Block 3 — AAR Facilitation (3 weeks)**
- AAR theory: what makes an AAR generate learning vs blame
- Facilitation techniques: question-led, self-assessment first, evidence-based challenge
- Multi-echelon AAR sequencing (platoon AARs feed company AAR)
- Cultural considerations: running AARs in Arabic / bilingual settings
- Non-punitive culture: how to create safety to fail in an assessment environment
- Practical: facilitate an AAR for a CTW exercise under supervision

**Block 4 — Allied OT Familiarisation (2 weeks)**
*Aligned with Q3 26 familiarisation visits already in SBTDWS timeline*
- Visit allied OT/observer trainer programmes (UK, US, Australian — one or two visits)
- Observe a live rotation or exercise at an allied CTC
- Debrief: what translates directly, what needs adaptation for ADWC context

**Output:** Candidates complete Phase 1 assessment. Those passing advance to Phase 2. Failures offered remediation or returned to parent unit.

---

### Phase 2 — Supervised Practice (Month 6–10)
*Q4 2026 — Q1 2027*

OT candidates begin operating in role, under close supervision of a Phase 3 senior mentor or allied OT advisor.

**Progression model:**
- Month 6: Shadow role — observe, record, attend AAR but do not lead
- Month 7–8: Co-OT role — share observation responsibilities; co-facilitate AAR under supervision
- Month 9–10: Primary OT role — lead observation and AAR; supervisor observes and assesses performance

**Assessed competencies during Phase 2:**
- Quality of event capture and evidence recording
- AAR facilitation quality (unit self-assessment, evidence use, non-punitive climate)
- Ability to sustain an adverse assessment under pushback from the assessed unit
- Situational awareness across echelons during live serial

**Supervision options (at least one required):**
- Allied OT advisor embedded during Phase 2 exercises (preferred)
- Experienced SBTDWS evaluator acting as senior mentor
- Video review of AARs with structured feedback

**Output:** Phase 2 assessment report per candidate. Candidates ready for Level 1 accreditation (platoon-level, supervised).

---

### Phase 3 — Formal Accreditation (Month 10–13)
*Q1 2027 — Q2 2027*

**Accreditation board:**
- Chaired by: SBTDWS Commandant or delegated O5
- Board members: SAT Cell (standards), Senior OT mentor, Allied OT advisor (if available)
- Assessment: observed live serial + AAR delivery + oral board on standards knowledge

**Authority to fail writ:**
*This must be signed BEFORE the first accredited validation serial — not after.*
- Commandant issues formal writ granting accredited OTs authority to issue adverse assessments
- Writ specifies: reporting chain, adverse assessment format, disagreement arbitration with PGI, command consequence for assessed units
- O6 CoC endorsement required — the authority to fail is only credible if it is backed by command

**First live validation serial (supervised):**
- Run concurrently with or immediately after first accreditations
- Company-level serial; SAG 9 or ADW validation focus
- All Level 2 OTs participate; senior mentor observes and debrief OTs (not the assessed unit)
- Output: first validation report in ADWC format

**IOC gate:** Minimum 8 Level 2 OTs accredited + authority to fail writ signed + first validation serial complete.

## Lead Times — What Takes How Long

| Activity | Duration | Why it takes this long |
|----------|----------|----------------------|
| Selection | 4–6 weeks | Board must be credible; wrong selections waste training investment |
| ADW competency profiles (SAT Cell) | 6–8 weeks | Novel work; requires J3 WDC input on SAG 9/10 standards |
| Foundation training | 12 weeks | Standards, methodology, facilitation — cannot be compressed without quality loss |
| Allied familiarisation visits | 2 weeks | Planning and coordination with partner; travel |
| Supervised practice | 16 weeks | Minimum exposure across enough exercise events to develop genuine competency |
| Accreditation board | 2–4 weeks | Board scheduling, live serial, oral boards |
| **Total minimum** | **~13 months** | With no delays and concurrent activity where possible |

**Contingency:** The 13-month pipeline has zero slack to reach Q2 27. Any slip in Phase 0 or Phase 1 pushes IOC to Q3 27 or beyond. Start now.

## IOC Definition

**ADWC OT Cadre IOC (Q2 2027):**
- Minimum 8 OTs accredited at Level 2
- ADW competency profiles published (v0.1 minimum)
- ADWC validation framework v0.1 published
- Authority to fail writ signed by Commandant, endorsed by O6 CoC
- One company-level validation serial completed in ADWC format
- Reporting chain confirmed (SBTDWS validation report → PG J7/J3)

**What IOC does NOT require:**
- Full cadre size (20+) — that is FOC
- Formation-level validation — that is FOC
- Hameem infrastructure — company-level validation can run on existing SBTDWS ground
- SAG 10 accreditation — SAG 9 is the priority; SAG 10 follows at FOC

---

## FOC Definition

**ADWC OT Cadre FOC (Q2 2028):**
- 20+ OTs across Levels 1–3
- At least 4 Level 3 Senior OTs capable of supervising others and chairing accreditation boards
- Self-sustaining pipeline: ADWC can accredit its own OTs without external supervision
- SAG 9 and SAG 10 both covered
- Formation-level validation serial capability (not just company)
- Multinational validation (partner/foreign forces OT accreditation pathway)
- Lessons library: structured AAR data feeding TEIW

## Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|--------|-----------|
| SAT Cell slips on ADW competency profiles | Medium | High — delays entire Phase 1 | Treat profiles as the first deliverable; Commandant priority; parallel draft with J3 WDC input |
| Selection produces wrong candidates | Medium | High — pipeline failures or weak OTs | Rigorous selection board; assess temperament not just rank; involve senior CTW officers |
| Allied familiarisation visits fall through | Low | Medium | Identify two options (UK and Australian); book early; paper-based case studies as fallback |
| Assessed units resist adverse assessments | High | High — destroys cadre credibility | Authority to fail writ must be signed before first serial; O6 must visibly back it in first adverse case |
| Cadre attrition (posting, promotion) | Medium | Medium — IOC with 8 OTs leaves no redundancy | Target 15–20 candidates to absorb 30–40% attrition; build Level 1 pipeline continuously |
| O5 resistance to OT authority | High | High — mid-management will test the cadre | Commandant must make clear in writing and in person that adverse assessments are binding |

## Relationship to Other Lines of Effort

**SAT Cell:** Produces ADW competency profiles (Phase 0 dependency). The SAT Cell transition to Doctrine and Training Authority directly enables the OT cadre — without standards, there is nothing to assess against.

**E&E Wing transition:** The OT cadre IS the E&E Wing's new identity. FST is phased out; E&E Wing staff become the OT cadre. This must be communicated as a role elevation, not a replacement.

**J3 WDC:** SAG 9/10 standards authority. OT training on SAG 9/10 must be agreed with J3 WDC to ensure the standards being trained are current. Force Generation Pipeline ToR (Q2 26) should include OT accreditation alignment.

**J7 (endorsed 2026-08-05):** [[J7 Readiness Flow and Deliverables Register]] C2 — "Observer Trainer Accreditation Standard," target Q4-26, owned by COMD J7 — is the formal instrument this whole plan's accreditation-standard dependency has been waiting on. The register states plainly: "SBT cannot train and grow an OT cadre without it." Same register's Readiness Flow also confirms SBT (this plan's home) as the party that "trains the OTC cadre to the J7 standard" — J7 sets the standard, SBT delivers the training.

**PGI/IWTC:** Disagreement arbitration. When OTs and PGI disagree on a competency call (individual vs collective), the arbitration process must be defined in the authority to fail writ before IOC.

**CTVMR Wing:** The OT cadre serves CTVMR. Cadre size must track exercise tempo — IOC supports one company serial at a time; FOC supports concurrent formation-level validation.

## Reference Models

| Centre | OT model | Relevant lesson |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| NTC | 600+ AARs per 28-day rotation; organised by battle function; 2-day classroom cert before each rotation | Volume of structured feedback is the output; OC/Ts are a production line, not a small team |
| JRTC | MOS-matched OC/Ts; JRTC Academy certification before duty | Match OT to the unit's specialty — a fires OT assessing a fires section produces better feedback |
| JMRC | OC/T Academy + deployable packages | Self-sustaining training pipeline is FOC criterion — ADWC must eventually accredit its own OTs |
| CTC-L (AUS) | OT cadre as core of the CTVMR equivalent | The OT cadre takes 18+ months to mature to full effectiveness in the Australian model |
