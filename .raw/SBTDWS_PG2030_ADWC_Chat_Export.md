# SBTDWS / PG 2030 / ADWC Transformation Chat Export

## 1. Project Purpose

The project is supporting **Saeed Bin Tahnoon Desert Warfare School (SBTDWS)** in interpreting and responding to the **PG 2030 / Al Emad** transformation direction.

The core challenge is to determine how SBTDWS can evolve from its current role as a desert warfare training school into a broader future institution that supports:

- Desert warfare expertise
- Readiness generation
- Collective training
- Validation
- Mission rehearsal
- Tactical experimentation
- Innovation integration
- Lessons-to-training feedback
- Support to PG 2030 / Al Emad proof-of-concept events

A key concern throughout the project is avoiding an over-ambitious or misleading label such as **All Domain Warfare Centre (ADWC)** if that title implies SBTDWS can independently deliver cyber, electromagnetic spectrum, space, C4I, digital, and all-domain enablers that remain dependent on wider PG structures, particularly J6 and other PG-level enablers.

---

## 2. Corrected PG 2030 Direction

The corrected PG 2030 direction states that SBTDWS is expected to expand from training PG force elements to operate in the desert environment into enabling them to prosecute **All Domain Warfare**.

The guidance describes the future role as enabling PG force elements to train and validate their ability to conduct operations that synchronise effects across:

- Land
- Air
- Sea
- Cyber
- Electromagnetic
- Space

The future label in the original guidance is **PG All Domain Warfare Centre (ADWC)** by 2030.

However, the discussion identified that this naming convention may create an **expectation gap** because SBTDWS cannot independently generate or own all-domain capabilities, especially cyber, EMS, space, secure C4I, data systems, and digital integration.

---

## 3. Corrected SBTDWS Indicative Tasks

The corrected guidance provided the following major indicative tasks for SBTDWS:

1. Develop the capacity and expertise to train and validate PG force elements to conduct All Domain operations that deliver synchronised effects across air, sea, land, cyber, electromagnetic, and space domains.
2. Hand off the existing SBTDWS capability development cell to the new PG Innovation Command.
3. When triggered by the new J3-led PG Warfare Development Centre, act as PG’s lead for tactical experimentation, testing operating concepts and developing effective TTPs prior to transferring them to PG units.
4. Integrate emerging operational trends and lessons learned from PG mission exploitation into training and course design.
5. Starting with **MAKHLAB 1**, support PG 2030 / Al Emad proof-of-concept events to showcase key elements of the transformation agenda.
6. Enhance the existing **Hameem** site to provide a world-class mission rehearsal facility for discreet, hyper-realistic, pre-deployment training.
7. Develop the capacity, knowledge, and expertise to exercise PG SRO responsibility for validating PG force elements’ ability to deliver **SAG 9 – Combined Unconventional Warfare** by 2030.
8. Develop the capacity, knowledge, and expertise to exercise PG SRO responsibility for validating PG force elements’ ability to deliver **SAG 10 – Resilience and Capacity Building** by 2030.
9. Ensure appropriate personnel attend IW training courses delivered by PGI, enabling them to be force-generated for partnered or combined special forces operations.

## 4. Higher PG 2030 Themes

The broader PG 2030 vision is framed around three themes:

### Adaptive by Nature

PG must evolve faster than adversaries, move beyond a narrow CT focus, and prepare for conventional, irregular, grey-zone, covert, clandestine, and deniable operations.

### Innovative by Design

Innovation is a cultural imperative, not merely a technical function. PG must encourage experimentation, accept failure in training environments, challenge old methods, rapidly adopt emerging capabilities, and integrate uncrewed and autonomous systems across domains.

### Constantly Ready

Readiness must become continuous and digitally enabled. PG must embed secure communications, data-enabled decision-making, cyber awareness, EMS awareness, integrated support, and FE@R-style readiness models across the force.

---

## 5. Core Analytical Conclusion

The project’s central conclusion is:

**SBTDWS should not simply be rebranded as an ADWC. It should evolve functionally into a readiness, validation, mission rehearsal, tactical experimentation, and lessons institution while preserving its Desert Warfare identity.**

The proposed future logic is:

**Train — Validate — Rehearse — Experiment — Feed Back**

This model allows SBTDWS to grow into the PG 2030 direction without pretending it can independently own or deliver all-domain effects.

A separate barriers paper was reviewed and incorporated into the analysis.

- Lack of constructive feedback
- Weak instructor accountability

- strengthen foundations
- mature standards

- improve assessment credibility
- enhance readiness assurance

Rather than bluntly stating that the system is failing.

## 8. Mission Analysis Summary

A detailed mission analysis was developed around the corrected PG 2030 direction.

### Mission in one sentence

**SBTDWS is being directed to evolve from a desert-environment training school into a broader PG readiness institution, with responsibility to prepare, rehearse, experiment, and validate PG force elements for future special operations, while remaining grounded in realistic desert training, mission rehearsal, and readiness generation.**

### Core operational problem

PG needs a future-ready special operations component that is adaptive, innovative, digitally enabled, and all-domain capable; however, SBTDWS in its current form remains primarily configured as a desert warfare training institution, not yet as a full readiness, rehearsal, and tactical experimentation system.

### Two problems to solve

1. **Design problem:** Current structure, authorities, functions, and external linkages are not yet aligned to the future end state.
2. **Performance problem:** Existing credibility, standards, assessment, resources, instructor development, and bureaucracy issues may limit transformation.

### Key CTC / JTW logic

- CTC HQ

## 15. Recommended Future Construct

The recommended future construct is:

### ADWC / Future Desert Warfare Centre HQ

Governance, priorities, standards, programme control, and external coordination.

### Desert Warfare Wing

The JTW-equivalent. Holds current courses, desert expertise, individual and small-team foundations.

### Collective Training and Validation Wing

The main effort. Delivers OPFOR, OT, EXCON, collective validation, SAG 9 / SAG 10 readiness activity.

### Mission Rehearsal and Hameem Wing

Develops and operates Hameem as a discreet, realistic, mission-specific rehearsal capability.

### Tactical Experimentation Cell

Executes J3 WDC-directed tactical experiments, MAKHLAB activity, TTP testing, UxS integration, and field experimentation.

### Battle Command / CPX Cell

Supports HQ training, staff processes, digital C2, task-force planning, and command drills.

### Standards, Lessons and AAR Cell

Owns assessment standards, AAR method, lesson capture, mission exploitation integration, validation reporting, and course redesign recommendations.

### Phase 3: Tactical Experimentation

Delivered by the Tactical Experimentation Cell.

Delivered at Hameem.

Delivered by Collective Training and Validation Wing plus Standards Cell.
