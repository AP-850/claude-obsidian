# Source: Army Pathway for Innovation and Technology (PIT)

Navigation: [[sources/_index]] | [[Research: Defense Innovation Governance Tools]]

The US Army's structured rapid innovation pathway, designed to bridge the valley of death between technology development and deployment.

---

## Three Operating Principles

1. Speed over perfection — prioritise rapid capability injection
2. Early Soldier feedback — ensure technologies meet actual warfighter needs
3. Demand validation — prove requirement before major investment

---

## Three Program Elements

| Element | Function |
|---------|---------|
| FUZE | Innovation engine using venture capital methods; identifies and scales from startups, academia, non-traditional performers |
| JIOP (Joint Integration and Operational Prototyping) | Rapid prototype testing in real-world battlefield conditions with embedded acquisition professionals |
| G-TEAD (Global Tactical Edge Acquisition Directorate) | Delivers combat-ready solutions within 90 days; empowers tactical commanders to influence needed capabilities |

---

## Governance Structure

Selection panels include the "big three":
- Portfolio Acquisition Executive / Program Manager
- PIT representative
- Warfighting unit representative

This triad ensures early evaluation for scalability, producibility, and manufacturability — preventing prototypes from failing during transition.

---

## Relevance to TACTIX

PIT's 3-element model maps directly onto a simplified TACTIX architecture:
- FUZE = Stage 1 (gap intake + market analysis)
- JIOP = Stage 4 (PoC and tactical experimentation design)
- G-TEAD = Stage 7 (scale decision with commander involvement)

"Speed over perfection" validates the recommendation to reduce TACTIX from 7 stages to 3. The 90-day delivery target suggests experiment workflows should be completable in 90 days or less.
