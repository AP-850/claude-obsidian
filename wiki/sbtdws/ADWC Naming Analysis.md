# ADWC Naming Analysis

Analysis of the naming challenge for SBTDWS's future identity. The formal directive uses "All Domain Warfare Centre" but this creates an expectation gap. This page documents the analysis and recommended alternatives.

Navigation: [[sbtdws/_index]] | [[ADWC Transformation Concept]] | [[ADWC FIC Analysis]]

---

## The Problem with "All Domain Warfare Centre"

The name "All Domain Warfare Centre" implies SBTDWS can independently deliver capability across all six domains: land, sea, air, cyber, electromagnetic, and space.

In practice:
- Land and air are accessible near-term
- Maritime is possible with Hameem beach access (limited)
- Cyber, EMS, and space remain dependent on J6 and wider PG enablers
- These cannot be generated by SBTDWS alone

The [[ADWC FIC Analysis]] names this the **Naming / Expectation Gap**: "All Domain Warfare Centre may create expectations beyond SBTDWS' direct control, especially for cyber, EMS, and space."

---

## The Core Finding

> **SBTDWS should not simply be rebranded as an ADWC. It should evolve functionally into a readiness, validation, mission rehearsal, tactical experimentation, and lessons institution — while preserving its Desert Warfare identity.**

The recommendation: **the formal name should preserve the Desert Warfare identity; the expanded remit should be expressed through mission, functions, and responsibilities, not through the title.**

## Recommended Alternative Names

| Name | Rationale |
|------|----------|
| **Saeed Bin Tahnoon Desert Warfare Centre** (top recommendation) | Preserves identity; centre implies broader mandate than school |
| Saeed Bin Tahnoon Desert Warfare and Readiness Centre | Adds readiness explicitly |
| Saeed Bin Tahnoon Desert Warfare and Mission Readiness Centre | Readiness + mission rehearsal implied |
| Saeed Bin Tahnoon Desert Warfare and Experimentation Centre | Emphasises tactical experimentation role |
| Saeed Bin Tahnoon Desert Warfare and Advanced Training Centre | Conservative; keeps training as primary identity |
| Saeed Bin Tahnoon Desert Warfare Centre of Excellence | Aligned to PG 2030 "Excellence" language |

---

## Why Preserve "Desert Warfare"

1. It is the genuine comparative advantage — SBTDWS owns this identity
2. The desert environment is the unique context that no other regional institution provides
3. Removing it signals discontinuity rather than evolution
4. It avoids overpromising on all-domain delivery

The central insight: **desert warfare remains the context; all-domain warfare becomes the purpose.** The name should reflect the context.

---

## Officer Briefing Line

> "The name 'All Domain Warfare Centre' creates the risk that we are promising capabilities that depend on J6, J3, and PG HQ to deliver. Retaining 'Desert Warfare' in the name while expressing the expanded mandate through our mission and functions is the more credible approach."

## Status

This analysis was developed but no formal naming decision has been recorded. The directive uses "All Domain Warfare Centre" as the 2030 end-state label. Any change to the name requires command authority above SBTDWS.

> [!contradiction] The name is already shifting in practice — "MDWC" (2026-07-28)
> [[MDWC Six-Domain Study]], a J7-authored study dated 2026-07-28, states outright: "ADWC (All Domain Warfare Centre) superseded by MDWC — Multi Domain Warfare Centre (Working Title)." This is not a formal D-10 decision — the study itself marks MDWC as a Working Title and is DRAFT, for HQ socialisation — but it is the first evidence of the name moving in an HQ-track document, from J7, above SBTDWS's own authority.
>
> The same day, [[sources/sbtdws-to-adwc-implementation-plan|SBTDWS's own Implementation Plan]] used "ADWC" throughout with no mention of MDWC. Two contemporaneous documents from two different authorities, using two different names — genuinely unresolved, not something to silently pick a side on.
>
> Worth noting: "Multi Domain" rather than "All Domain" is itself a partial, independent validation of this page's core critique — MDWC's Six-Domain Study explicitly scopes space and cyber **out** of the institution's remit, addressing the same overpromising risk this analysis raised, but via a different fix than the "keep Desert Warfare, drop domain language" recommendation above. See [[Commander Decisions Register]] D-10 for the decision-register cross-reference.
