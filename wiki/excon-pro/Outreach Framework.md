# EXCON Pro — Outreach Framework

Two groups, two different conversations. The outreach message, the conversation structure, and the ask are different for each.

Navigation: [[excon-pro/_index]] | [[Expert Shortlist]]

---

## GROUP 1 — Military Exercise Directors and J7 Training Staff

### Who they are

Currently serving or recently separated J7 officers, exercise directors, and training officers who have personally planned and run exercises. They know the documentation burden first-hand. They are the end-user, not necessarily the budget holder.

### What they validate

Operational relevance. Does this solve a real problem in the way exercises actually run? Does the output meet doctrine? Would their team trust it?

### What they do NOT care about (yet)

Pricing, business model, competitive landscape. Keep the commercial conversation out of Group 1 entirely. They are validators, not buyers at this stage.

---

### Outreach Message — Group 1

> Subject: Exercise planning tool — honest feedback request
>
> [Name],
>
> I'm supporting a colleague who has built an AI-powered exercise planning system — it generates a complete 24-document package, from General Idea to Role Player Briefs, in around 30 minutes. NATO doctrine throughout, real-world intelligence integration via ACLED and GDELT, 21 AI agents auditing every document for doctrine compliance and inject logic before you see it.
>
> We're at early feedback stage and looking specifically for people who have actually planned and run exercises — not to sell anything, but to find out what's wrong with it.
>
> Would you be willing to give us 30–45 minutes? We'd show you the product and ask you to tell us honestly where it breaks, what's missing, and whether it would survive contact with a real exercise planning team.
>
> [Your name]

**Notes on tone:**
- No buzzwords. No "revolutionary" or "game-changing."
- The ask is explicit: honest feedback, not a sales conversation.
- Lead with the time saving (30 minutes vs weeks) — that is the hook for this audience.
- Warm approach preferred: mutual contact introduction > LinkedIn cold > email cold.

---

### Conversation Guide — Group 1

**Opening (5 minutes — before showing anything)**

Do not open with the product. Open with the problem.

> "Before I show you anything, I want to understand your current process. Walk me through how your team builds a full exercise document package — from the General Idea through to the player briefs. What does that actually look like?"

Let them talk. Listen for: time estimates, who does the work, where it breaks, what they complain about. This is your baseline and it tells you which capabilities to emphasise in the demo.

---

**Core Questions (20 minutes)**

1. **The documentation burden**
   > "How long does the full documentation package take your team to produce for a major exercise? Who carries that load?"

   *Listen for:* Weeks vs months. Whether it falls on one or two people. Whether it gets rushed as the exercise date approaches.

2. **Doctrine and quality failures**
   > "Have you ever had injects fail during execution because of inconsistencies in the documents — wrong unit references, doctrine errors, inject logic that didn't hold up? What happened?"

   *Listen for:* Stories of last-minute corrections, exercise credibility failures, embarrassment in front of senior observers. These are the moments the QA pipeline prevents.

3. **Scenario realism**
   > "How do you currently handle scenario design — are your exercises based on real-world environments and current events, or do you use fictional backgrounds? What are the tradeoffs?"

   *Listen for:* Frustration with fictional scenarios feeling stale or unrealistic. Enthusiasm for real-world grounding — or concern about classification/sensitivity implications.

4. **Lessons capture**
   > "What does your AAR lessons capture process look like? How often do those lessons actually make it into the next exercise design?"

   *Listen for:* The honest answer is usually "we write it down and it never gets used." The Lessons Library directly addresses this — let them describe the problem before you show the solution.

5. **The time saving test**
   > "If you could produce a doctrine-compliant, fully coherent document package in 30 minutes — what would that actually change for your team? What would you do with that time?"

   *Listen for:* Whether they believe it is possible. Scepticism here is good — it tells you what you need to prove in the demo.

6. **Security and classification (critical)**
   > "One thing we want to get right — what classification level does your team typically work at when designing exercises? What would you need to know about data handling before you'd trust a system like this?"

   *Listen for:* This is non-negotiable for military audiences. Have the answer to this question prepared by your colleague before the conversation. If you don't have it, be honest: "That's one of the things we're clarifying — I'll come back to you on it."

---

**Demo (if available — 10 minutes)**

Show the 24-document package output first. That is the most immediately legible capability.
Then show the QA pipeline — explain that 21 agents audited the document before it was presented.
Then show the live intelligence integration — this is the "wow" moment for scenario designers.

---

**Closing (5 minutes)**

> "Based on what you've seen — what's wrong with it? What would you need to see to trust it with a real exercise?"

Then the ask:

> "We're looking for a small group of practitioners who'd be willing to run a real scenario through it and give us detailed feedback over a few sessions. Would that be something you'd consider?"

This is the conversion from "conversation" to "structured beta reviewer." Not everyone will say yes — but the people who do are your most valuable validators.

---

### Key Objections — Group 1

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "How do I know the AI output meets doctrine?" | 21 specialist agents audit every document before output. Show the QA pipeline. |
| "What classification level does it operate at?" | Confirm with your colleague before the conversation. Do not guess. |
| "My team won't trust AI-generated injects" | Fair. That's exactly why we want practitioners testing it — you set the quality bar, not us. |
| "We already have a process" | "How long does it take? What breaks?" Let them describe the pain themselves. |
| "What happens to our exercise data?" | Data hosting and sovereignty question — needs a confirmed answer from your colleague. |

## GROUP 2 — Veterans in Defence Commercial and SaaS

### Who they are

Former military (typically officer level) who have moved into defence industry, tech, or investment. They understand both the operational problem and the commercial landscape. They give frank feedback without the institutional defensiveness of active-duty audiences.

### What they validate

Commercial viability. Is there a real market? Who is the actual buyer? What does the growth path look like? Where are the landmines? What would they need to see to back this or recommend it?

### What they do NOT care about (primarily)

Whether the documents are doctrine-compliant. They trust you on the product. Their lens is commercial and strategic.

---

### Outreach Message — Group 2

> Subject: Honest commercial read on a defence SaaS product
>
> [Name],
>
> I'm supporting a colleague who has built what I believe is a genuinely differentiated product — an AI-powered exercise planning system that automates the full documentation package for military and emergency services exercises. 21 AI agents auditing every document, real-world intelligence integration, NATO doctrine throughout. The time saving is real: 30 minutes vs weeks manually.
>
> We're at early feedback stage and I'm specifically looking for people who have been on both sides — operational and commercial. Not for a pitch, but for an honest pressure-test of our market assumptions.
>
> Would you give me 30 minutes? I want your commercial brain, not a polite reception.
>
> [Your name]

**Notes on tone:**
- Peer-to-peer. You are approaching them as equals.
- "Honest pressure-test" signals you want real feedback, not validation.
- "Commercial brain" signals you want their commercial perspective, which is more valuable to them to give than their military perspective.

---

### Conversation Guide — Group 2

**Opening (5 minutes)**

> "I want your honest commercial read on this. I'm not looking for encouragement — I'm looking for where this breaks commercially, what we haven't thought of, and who the right people to talk to are. That's more useful to us than a positive reception."

Setting this frame upfront gets you the useful conversation, not the polite one.

---

**Core Questions (20 minutes)**

1. **Market reality check**
   > "Have you encountered the exercise planning documentation problem in your time in uniform? How did units handle it — and do you believe there's genuine willingness to pay to solve it?"

   *Listen for:* Whether they validate the problem, or whether they think units will just keep doing it manually because it's "always been done that way." Cultural resistance to change is the most common landmine in defence SaaS.

2. **The buyer question**
   > "In your experience, who actually controls the budget for training technology at unit or formation level? Is it J7, the training establishment CO, procurement, or somewhere else? And what does the procurement cycle look like?"

   *This is critical.* The end-user (exercise director) and the buyer (budget holder) are often different people. Knowing who signs the contract shapes the entire go-to-market.

3. **Commercial model pressure-test**
   > "If you were evaluating this as an investor or advisor — what metrics would you want to see at six months? What does a healthy early-stage defence SaaS look like to you?"

   *Listen for:* Their mental model of what success looks like — pilots, contracts, ARR, reference customers. This tells you how sophisticated their commercial lens is.

4. **Competitive landscape**
   > "Who else is trying to solve this problem? What's your read on the competitive landscape — existing exercise planning tools, simulation companies, general AI productivity tools entering this space?"

   *Listen for:* Named competitors you haven't considered. Large incumbents who might acquire or copy. The "good enough" free alternative (Word + experience).

5. **The landmine question**
   > "What's the thing we haven't thought of? Regulatory, procurement, cultural, security — what's the landmine that kills defence SaaS products that look promising on paper?"

   *Listen for:* Data sovereignty issues, export control complications, security clearance requirements for the platform, procurement cycle length mismatched with startup runway, institutional resistance from existing suppliers.

6. **Scale and moat**
   > "If this product is as capable as it appears — what's the one thing that would prevent adoption at scale? And what would give it a defensible moat once a large organisation has embedded it?"

   *Listen for:* Their view on the Lessons Library as a compounding moat (data advantage over time). Whether they see the NATO standards as a differentiator or a constraint.

7. **The network ask**
   > "Who are the two or three people you'd put in front of this product immediately — either as validators or as potential early customers?"

   *This is the highest-value output of the Group 2 conversation.* One warm introduction from a trusted veteran is worth ten cold outreach attempts.

---

**Closing (5 minutes)**

> "We're building a small advisory group — people who'd be willing to stay close to this over the next six months and give us honest commercial feedback as we iterate. Based on this conversation, would that be something you'd consider?"

This is the conversion from "conversation" to "informal advisor." It is a light ask — no formal commitment, no equity discussion at this stage. Just continued access and honest feedback.

---

### Key Objections — Group 2

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Defence procurement cycles are too long for a startup" | "Agreed — that's one of the things we want your read on. How have you seen other products navigate that?" Turn it into a question. |
| "The market is too small" | "Walk me through your sizing. We see [military + emergency services + joint ops] as the initial TAM — where does your estimate differ?" |
| "Large incumbents will just copy this" | "Possibly. What do you think the window is, and what does the defensible version of this look like?" |
| "AI in defence is a regulatory minefield" | "Where specifically? Data sovereignty, export control, or something else?" Pin down the specific risk. |
| "Who's backing this?" | Be honest about where you are: early stage, founder-led, seeking initial validation before formalising structure. |

## Sequencing Recommendation

**Week 1–2 (now, before full read-in):**
- Build the shortlist: 8–10 Group 1 names, 6–8 Group 2 names
- Confirm answers to the 5 open questions in [[excon-pro/_index]] with your colleague
- Draft personalised versions of the outreach messages for your top 5 contacts

**Week 3–4 (after read-in, with demo access):**
- Begin Group 1 outreach — aim for 5 conversations in the first round
- Begin Group 2 outreach in parallel — aim for 3–4 conversations
- Run conversations using the guides above

**Week 5–6:**
- Synthesise feedback across both groups
- Identify 2–3 practitioners willing to become structured beta reviewers
- Identify 1–2 veterans willing to act as informal advisors
- Brief your colleague on findings with specific product and commercial implications

---

## What Success Looks Like at Six Weeks

- 5+ Group 1 conversations completed with structured notes
- 3+ Group 2 conversations completed with structured notes
- At least one practitioner willing to run a real scenario through the product
- At least one named warm introduction to a potential early customer or investor
- A clear answer to: "Is the 30-minutes claim credible to people who've actually done this work?"
