# ADWC FIC Analysis

Functions, Institutions, Capabilities analysis for SBTDWS transformation. First produced May 2026. Fully ingested from Excel (`SBT PG 2030 - FIC analysis_CH_updated.xlsx`) on 2026-06-10. Covers 8 FIC inputs across 9 indicative tasks, 8 work groupings, and 8 change log amendments.

Navigation: [[sbtdws/_index]] | [[ADWC Transformation Concept]] | [[SBTDWS Key Relationships]] | [[SBTDWS Action Plan]]

> [!key-insight] The core finding
> SBTDWS can build many parts of the future model, but it cannot deliver the full 2030 role alone. The main requirement is to connect the school to the right command authorities, J-Staff enablers, training architecture, and operational units.

## 2. Key Planning Observations by FIC Input

### Organisation / Structure
- T1: SBTDWS requires an endorsed future operating concept before any external ADWC adoption. Future structure should separate training delivery, validation, experimentation, mission rehearsal, and enabling support — not simply rename the school.
- T3: SBTDWS should be the field execution arm for tactical experimentation, with J3 WDC owning governance and doctrine feedback.
- T4: A lessons function or OPEX feeder must be formally embedded; relying on ad hoc course updates will not keep pace with operational change.
- T5: POC events need a single accountable lead nested in PG 2030 governance; otherwise showcase activity competes with transformation delivery.
- T6: Hameem requires a dedicated mission rehearsal development workstream with J7 architectural authority and J8 capital planning integration.
- T7: SAG 9 responsibility needs a clear split between SRO, training provider, validator, and operational command; UW raising construct remains a critical uncertainty.
- T8: SAG 10 requires clarity on whether SBTDWS validates R&CB as a training outcome, an operational effect, or both.
- T9: The PGI-SBTDWS split must be formalised — PGI owns individual IW training; SBTDWS builds collective readiness and validation.

### Personnel
- T1: Current capacity cannot support all-domain training without allocated J-staff, reach-back SMEs, and deliberate use of low-tempo windows.
- T3: Needs a small experimentation cell able to design serials, capture data, run AARs, and translate findings into TTP recommendations.
- T4: Requires staff with analytical skill, recent operational credibility, and authority to challenge stale course content. Ramadan/summer windows should be programmed for course redesign and lessons integration.
- T6: Needs HICON/EXCON staff, scenario designers, LVC technicians, range control, facilities management, and CIS support — beyond normal instructor manning.
- T7: Requires UW-experienced instructors, OTs, planners, linguists/culture SMEs, and PGI/IWTC pipeline-selected personnel.
- T8: Requires SMEs in partner-force training, civil-military engagement, resilience, capacity building, and assessment — not only desert warfare instructors.

### Collective Training Pathway
- T1: Requires a codified pipeline from individual qualification to collective validation and FE@R reporting; without it, all-domain training remains a set of disconnected courses.
- T3: Experimentation must be deliberately linked to training design; otherwise TTPs will be demonstrated but not institutionalised.
- T4: Lessons must drive course review, validation criteria, and scenario updates on a routine cycle — not as occasional post-deployment anecdotes.
- T5: POC events should demonstrate capability progression against standards, not become disconnected technology displays.
- T6: Mission rehearsal sits at the top of the training continuum and feeds validation, FE@R, and readiness reporting.
- T7/T8: Validation outputs must include evidence-based findings and a remediation pathway. Findings must be linked to remediation or readiness reporting.

### Major Systems
- T1: All-domain training depends on LVC, C4I, cyber/EMS overlays, and instrumentation. SBTDWS cannot self-generate the digital backbone.
- T3: Makhlab 1 needs an operating model that can test UxS, sensors, comms, EW/EMS effects, and decision support without over-promising maturity.
- T4: A simple lessons capture and reporting system is required before more complex data tooling is pursued.
- T5: Immature systems should be framed as experimentation, not capability.
- T6: Requires instrumentation, EXCON tools, comms, simulation, digital backbone connectivity, and data capture aligned to J7 LVC architecture.
- T8/T9: Evidence capture and digital AAR matter more than platforms for validation credibility.

### Supplies / Logistics
- T1: Repeatable all-domain training requires a controlled training stores model for specialist equipment, batteries, UxS consumables, spares, and range support.
- T3: Tactical experimentation will consume specialist spares and consumables; rapid repair/replace pathways needed to maintain tempo.
- T6: Operating Hameem at scale requires J4, medical, and sustainment input covering funding, maintenance contracts, consumables, fuel, water, CASEVAC, recovery, transport, and comms sustainment.
- T7: UW training may require bespoke stores, low-signature sustainment, role-player support, and careful inventory control.

### Facilities
- T1: Training areas and classrooms must support planning, simulation, rehearsal, and validation. Hameem is pivotal but not sufficient alone.
- T3: Hameem can become the experimentation venue, but only if instrumented and governed; early serials may need manual data capture.
- T6: Current Hameem is a training area, not an enabled mission rehearsal capability. Development requires range authority, safety governance, scheduling control, instrumentation approvals, security, LVC integration, and phased capital scope control.
- T7: Needs discrete training areas and mission rehearsal environments suitable for UW scenarios; OPSEC, CI, access control, and signature management must be built into the facility model.
- T9: PGI delivers the individual course; SBTDWS needs follow-on environments to consolidate and validate collective application.

### Support
- T1: Requires J7, J6, J3 WDC, and PGI support to avoid SBTDWS becoming an isolated training provider with an inflated title.
- T3: Requires STC, J3 WDC, J6, J8, and operational unit participation; otherwise experimentation remains school-level innovation.
- T4: Requires J2, mission exploitation, threat analysis, and operational unit support to feed adversary TTPs, red-team input, lessons, and readiness gaps.
- T6: Requires PG HQ, J7, J6, J8, Hameem/range authorities, Security/OPSEC/CI, and contractor/industry support. SBTDWS cannot build or sustain this independently.
- T7: SOC handover, PGI/IWTC, Component Command, J3/J7 standards, and Security/OPSEC/CI are all gating dependencies.

### Command & Management
- T1: COMD PG must decide whether ADWC is a true PG-level capability node or a renamed school. The title creates an expectation gap unless domain ownership, dependencies, and reporting authorities are confirmed.
- T3: Experimentation trigger, selection, approval, reporting, and output ownership must be clear before Makhlab 1 becomes a precedent for informal activity. J3 WDC should own priorities and TTP transfer.
- T4: The Steering Committee should mandate a lessons-to-training governance loop and protect honest reporting so deficiencies can be identified without reputational pressure overriding readiness truth.
- T5: POC events should be decision points for resource allocation and governance — not only demonstrations for senior visitors.
- T6: Hameem decisions should be treated as PG-level capital and readiness decisions, with J7 LVC alignment, J6 digital dependency, security requirements, and scope control to avoid an unfunded wish list.
- T7: The UW unit raising decision must be escalated early. SAG 9 authority must include standards, reporting chain, and power to assess, certify, or recommend remediation.
- T8: Higher HQ must define standards and reporting authority for SAG 10 — including whether SBTDWS can assess, report, certify, or recommend remediation.
- T9: A joint PGI-SBTDWS force generation mechanism is required so IW-trained personnel are employed in the roles they were generated for.

## 6. Key Issues for Command Direction

1. **IW / UW clarification required.** Higher HQ must confirm the IW / UW distinction before SBTDWS can define its validation scope.
2. **Naming risk.** "All Domain Warfare Centre" may create an expectation gap — cyber, EMS, space are not independently deliverable near-term.
3. **Validation authority.** SAG 9 / SAG 10 validation requires endorsed standards and authority to report honestly, with power to assess, certify, or recommend remediation.
4. **Do not over-commit.** The direction is flexible — SBTDWS should use that flexibility, not fill it with promises it cannot yet keep.
5. **Use low-tempo periods.** Ramadan and summer windows are development opportunities — instructor training, course redesign, simulation planning.
6. **Single point of failure risk.** SAG 9/10 SRO + UW raising mission + FDC 5 tactical experimentation lead + Hameem LVC delivery all currently sit on SBTDWS without sufficient resource.
7. **Define Hameem early.** Hameem must be planned as a mission rehearsal capability — EXCON, LVC integration, data capture, scenario design from the start.
8. **Build feedback loops.** Mission lessons and unit feedback must feed directly into training design, TTP refinement, and validation standards.
