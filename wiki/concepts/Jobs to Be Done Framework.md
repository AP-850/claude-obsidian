# Jobs to Be Done Framework

A product design framework that defines a product's purpose by the specific "job" a user hires it to do — not by its features. Developed by Clayton Christensen; Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI) variant by Tony Ulwick.

Navigation: [[Research: Defense Innovation Governance Tools]] | [[Output-First Product Design]]

---

## Core Idea

Users don't buy products; they hire them to do a job. The job is a specific goal in a specific context:

> "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."

Products that nail the job win. Products that accumulate features without addressing the job lose to simpler tools that do the job well.

---

## Failure Modes

1. Implementation disconnect — insights collected but not translated into product decisions
2. Organisational silos — only one team owns the framework; product teams don't see relevance
3. Resistance to change — feature-driven teams resist reframing around jobs

---

## Applied to TACTIX

**The job TACTIX should be hired to do:**

> "When I've run a capability trial, I want to structure my findings and recommendation, so I can brief my commander and get a decision within 48 hours."

This job statement has three implications:
1. The user is post-trial, not pre-trial (the tool supports reporting, not just planning)
2. The output is a command brief, not a comprehensive record
3. Speed is explicit — 48-hour turnaround, not a multi-week documentation exercise

**Secondary jobs** (which could be separate product features):
- "When I'm identifying a capability gap, I want to know what COTS solutions exist, so I can avoid building what I can buy." (Stage 2 — Market Analysis)
- "When I'm designing an experiment, I want a pre-built scenario template, so I can start in 30 minutes." (Stage 4 — PoC Design)
- "When a trial fails, I want to capture the lesson, so it doesn't happen again in another unit." (Stage 6 — Lessons Register)

The primary job drives the MVP. Secondary jobs drive Phase 2.

---

## Relevance

Applying JTBD to the current 16-section TACTIX: most sections serve the secondary jobs. The primary job (commander brief, 48-hour turnaround) requires at most 4-5 sections. This is the redesign case.
