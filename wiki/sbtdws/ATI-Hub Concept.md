# ATI-Hub — All Domain Technology and Innovation Hub

The ATI-Hub is a dedicated, commander-led innovation and emerging-technology capability for the Desert Warfare School (DWS). It accelerates the development, testing, and fielding of operationally relevant systems, combining rapid prototyping, tactical evaluation, FPV/UxS capability generation, and accelerated fielding pathways.

## Strategic Rationale

Modern conflict demonstrates that technological advantage is no longer solely held by major powers. Small, agile actors can leverage autonomous systems, FPV drones, AI-enabled sensors, and rapid prototyping to create disproportionate effect.

DWS must shift from being a training institution to becoming an ecosystem for continuous capability generation — creating practical combat multipliers tailored to the desert environment and rapidly fieldable across the force.

**The gap the ATI-Hub solves:**
PG currently lacks a permanent mechanism to:
- Convert operational problems into solutions at speed
- Experiment safely in realistic environments
- Bridge prototype to fielded capability ("valley of death")
- Integrate operators, industry, and academia efficiently
- Retain discretion, sovereignty, and resilience in key technologies (UxS, FPV)

## Governance Structure

### Innovation Command (ATI-Hub parent)
A new formation under uniform OF-5 leadership, reporting directly to the DWS Commander. Mandate: direct, coordinate, and certify all innovation, prototyping, and UxS capability development.

**Three core wings:**

| Wing | Role |
|------|------|
| Research and Development Wing (RDW) | Civilian engineers, scientists, technicians, SMEs — design, prototype, fabricate |
| Test, Evaluation and Experimentation Wing (TEX-Wing) | Senior advisors, operators, TTP developers — live trials, tactical assessments, lessons capture |
| UxS and FPV Capability Wing | Build and train operational UxS/FPV teams; deliver TTPs, sustainment plans, fielding packages |

**Supporting Cells:**

| Cell | Role |
|------|------|
| Transition and Commercialisation Cell | Contracting, IP, non-attribution design, HOTO to primes, acquisition pathways |
| Engagement and Ecosystem Cell | Innovation challenges, industry days, university partnerships, international liaison |
| Advisory Board (Quarterly) | Multi-domain oversight: command, acquisition, industry, academia, legal, technical |

## Facility Concept

**Location: Off-camp** — enables easy civilian, startup, and international partner access; faster contracting and procurement; "front door" for defence problems.

**Core infrastructure:**
- Advanced fabrication labs (3D printing, composites, CNC, electronics)
- AI/ML development suite
- Prototype assembly bays for UAS/UGV
- Desert-specific environmental testing rigs
- Secure classified annex for sensitive projects
- FPV drone training lanes, nets, and tactical ranges
- Co-working and partner innovation spaces

## Operating Model — 7-Phase Pipeline

Based on international best practice (SOFWERX, DIU, NATO DIANA, UK BattleLab patterns — referenced without naming in the published concept paper).

### Phase 1 — Problem Definition (Twice-Yearly)
Units and higher command submit prioritised problem statements. Each receives:
- A Problem Sponsor (unit commander)
- A User Champion (SNCO/junior officer) embedded with RDW teams

This prevents the most common failure mode: "interesting technology with no customer."

### Phase 2 — Sprint Events (3–5 days)
Operators, engineers, and industry co-develop early concepts in short, intense sprints. Outputs: shared understanding, rapid convergence, visible momentum for command.

### Phase 3 — Prototype Development (First 90 Days)
RDW produces functional prototypes: 3D models, electronics boards, flight/drive control integration, first-flight/first-drive prototypes.

### Phase 4 — Tactical Evaluation and TTP Generation
TEX-Wing conducts structured field trials:
- Environmental and endurance testing
- Failure modes and safety validation
- Tactical employment experiments
- Integration with fires, ISR, and C2

Every trial produces a TEX Report contributing to a continuously updated DWS UxS Doctrine Annex.

### Phase 5 — Transition Gate
Formal gate requiring:
- Sponsor unit acceptance
- DLOD/FIC compliance
- Fielding dossier (doctrine, training, sustainment, infrastructure, organisation, personnel, C2)
- Cost and production readiness

### Phase 6 — Production (0–100 Units)
ATI-Hub maintains ability to produce up to 100 units of any given system. Enables:
- Rapid fielding and contingency stockpiling
- Non-attribution and signature management
- Early iteration before a prime takes over

### Phase 7 — HOTO to Prime
Transition Cell handles commercialisation and licensing for a partner company to scale production.

## DLOD/FIC Fielding Dossier Standard

Every ATI-Hub output requires a fielding dossier covering all eight lines of development:

| DLOD Element | What It Covers |
|-------------|---------------|
| Doctrine | TTPs, integration with fires/C2/EW, employment concept |
| Organisation | Which unit owns what; manning deltas |
| Training | Pipeline, refresher, simulation support, qualification standards |
| Materiel | Standard parts, spares, battery policy, packaging |
| Infrastructure | Charging stations, workshops, storage, test lanes |
| Personnel | MOS/role descriptions, manning deltas |
| Sustainment | Repair times, stock levels, upgrade cycles, tech refresh triggers |
| C2 and Information | BMS integration, legal/ROE, data storage |

This is the differentiator: most innovation hubs prototype; they don't own the DLOD/FIC thinking. ATI-Hub owns both ends.

## 24-Month Initial Capability Focus

| Horizon | Focus |
|---------|-------|
| 70% | Improving current capability and training |
| 20% | Emerging technologies with near-term impact |
| 10% | Disruptive concepts for future advantage |

**Operational/Strategic UxS:** Long-range autonomy, discreet surveillance, persistent overwatch, high-endurance desert UAV
**Tactical/Strike UxS:** Loitering effects, mid-range FPV strike, autonomous desert resupply UGVs, mesh-network comms nodes
**Specialist/Discreet:** Ultra-low-signature drones, concealed UGV sensors, micro-UAS, non-attributable payloads
**FPV Wing (Reserve Brigade):** Ten 10-man FPV teams, standardised syllabus, organic production

## Strategic Edge (Pitch to Higher Command)

Five differentiators versus other nations' innovation hubs:
1. **Focused operational domain** — desert warfare, UxS, FPV, discreet capabilities in extreme heat
2. **Full DLOD/FIC ownership** — doesn't just prototype; delivers fieldable, doctrinally integrated capabilities
3. **Organic 0–100 unit production** — sovereign resilience, non-attribution, rapid fielding
4. **Reserve Brigade integration** — standing FPV and UxS talent pipeline
5. **Off-camp but command-led** — best of BattleLab and SOFWERX under OF-5 Innovation Commander tied into DWS
