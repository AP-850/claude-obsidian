## 1. Purpose

This plan translates the 9 Paper 2 indicative tasks into a phased, realistic implementation roadmap. It is organised around three objectives derived from J3 guidance. For each objective it identifies: current state, end state, phase-by-phase actions, personnel requirements, system and infrastructure requirements, authority requirements, dependencies, and risks.

---

## 2. Framing Guidance (J3, 2026-06-10)

- Desert warfare remains the core identity and focus.
- Develop the school slowly, in line with the broader PG units tempo — do not outpace the force.
- Domain priority: land and air. Scope maritime, space, cyber, and EMS progressively if required.
- Main effort: collective training, validation, and mission rehearsal.
- Supporting effort: courses and individual training — review, consolidate, do not expand.
- Each objective requires a detailed Action Plan ready for implementation.

## 4. Objective 1: Collective Training, Validation, and Mission Rehearsal

**Work Groupings:** WG1, WG3, WG5, WG6

### 4.1 Current State

SBTDWS currently delivers individual and small-unit desert warfare training. There is no collective training pipeline, no accredited OT cadre, no EXCON or HICON capability, no validation authority, and no endorsed SAG 9/10 standards. Hameem exists as a training area, not a mission rehearsal facility. Assessment culture is undermined by inflated pass rates and weak instructor accountability — the institutional floor must be raised before higher validation functions can be credible.

### 4.2 End State (2030)

- Codified collective training pipeline: individual qualification → collective training → mission rehearsal → FE@R reporting.
- Accredited OT cadre at FOC: 20+ OTs across Levels 1–3, including 4+ Senior OTs capable of supervising others and chairing accreditation boards. Self-sustaining — SBTDWS can accredit its own OTs without external support.
- SAG 9 validation operational. SAG 10 developing.
- Authority confirmed: SBTDWS can assess, certify, or recommend remediation — not advisory only.
- Hameem at FOC: world-class, instrumented mission rehearsal facility with EXCON, LVC integration, data capture, and EW/cyber injects.
- EXCON and HICON operational and integrated with J7 LVC architecture.
- Honest assessment culture embedded: adverse assessments are issued, backed by command, and drive remediation.

## 6. Objective 3: Training and Course Management

**Work Groupings:** WG3, WG6, WG7

### 6.1 Current State

SBTDWS runs a range of individual and collective courses, several of which are partially contractor-delivered. Assessment culture is undermined by inflated pass rates and a tendency to preserve the appearance of success over honest readiness reporting. There is no systematic lessons integration cycle and no formalised pipeline with PGI/IWTC for IW force generation. This is a supporting effort — the goal is a leaner, more credible, military-owned course portfolio that feeds into Objective 1.

### 6.2 End State (2030)

- Consolidated course portfolio: focused on what SBTDWS can deliver credibly and what feeds collective training.
- Military instructor ownership of all core military skills — contractor delivery reduced to specialist or enabling roles only.
- Formal lessons integration cycle: exercise observations, operational lessons, and unit feedback feed routine course review.
- PGI/SBTDWS pipeline formalised: IW-trained personnel are tracked, employed, and their collective application is consolidated and validated at SBTDWS.
- Honest assessment culture: pass rates reflect genuine standards, not social pressure.

These apply across all three objectives and cannot be managed within a single objective lane.

See [[Commander Decisions Register]] for full decision log and O6 actions required.

### 7.2 Structure and J-Staff Enablement (WG2)

The SBTDWS structure must be reorganised around functions, not legacy titles:

| Function | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Training Delivery | Individual and collective courses; supporting effort under J3 guidance |
| Validation | OT cadre, EXCON, HICON, assessment; main effort |
| Tactical Experimentation | TEIW, Makhlab series, TTP development; main effort |
| Mission Rehearsal | Hameem; pre-deployment rehearsal; main effort |
| Enabling Support | J-staff, CIS, logistics, admin |

Structural reorganisation plan must be endorsed Q4 2026. Reorganisation cannot precede the 6GW training review.

### 7.3 Resources, Sustainment, and Risk (WG8)

A consolidated resource ask covering all three objectives must be compiled and submitted. Key resource requirements across objectives:

| Resource | Objective | Phase | Scale |
|----------|-----------|-------|-------|
| Additional personnel (OTs, EXCON, cell) | 1 | Phase 1 | 30–45 total at FOC |
| Hameem capital investment | 1 | FY27–FY30 | TBC — PG-level capital decision |
| UxS equipment portfolio | 2 | Phase 1 | FPV, VTOL, land — numbers per TEIW charter |
| Digital AAR and instrumentation | 1, 2 | Phase 1/2 | J6 scoping required |
| Instructor development programme | 3 | PREP | Internal; low-cost if using existing low-tempo windows |

### 7.4 Single Point of Failure Risk

As noted in [[ADWC FIC Analysis]]: SAG 9/10 SRO responsibility + UW raising + FDC 5 tactical experimentation + Hameem LVC delivery all sit on SBTDWS simultaneously. If the school is under-resourced, all lines of effort are at risk at the same time. The resource submission (WG8) must make this risk explicit.
