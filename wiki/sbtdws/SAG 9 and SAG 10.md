# SAG 9 and SAG 10

Study and Evaluation Groups 9 and 10 are PG Command-level tasks that SBTDWS is directed to support as the Senior Responsible Owner (SRO) for validation. They define the two highest-stakes validation responsibilities SBTDWS must deliver by 2030.

Navigation: [[sbtdws/_index]] | [[PG 2030 Directive]] | [[OC-T Cadre Development Plan]]

---

## What Are Study and Evaluation Groups?

SAGs (Study and Evaluation Groups) are PG Command task frameworks — designated study areas that units are responsible for evaluating, developing capability for, and reporting on. Being assigned SRO responsibility means SBTDWS is the lead for validating PG force elements against that standard.

---

## SAG 9 — Irregular Warfare (Joint)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Full title | Study and Evaluation Group No. 9 — Irregular Warfare (Joint) |
| SBTDWS role | SRO — validate PG force elements for IW (Joint) tasks |
| Current status | GAP (Task 2 in operationally-interpreted mapping) |
| Current constraint | No trained OT cadre; no validation authority; no accreditation standard |

SAG 9 requires SBTDWS to develop the capability to support Higher Command in ensuring PG units can execute joint irregular warfare tasks. This includes combined unconventional warfare, partnered operations, and special joint tasks.

### IW / UW distinction (unresolved)

> [!gap] IW vs UW clarification required
> Higher HQ direction is needed to confirm the difference between: (a) the Pl(-) Irregular Warfare capability being grown across all PG units, and (b) any separate Unconventional Warfare capability handed over from SOC. This directly affects what SBTDWS validates and what PGI / IWTC delivers at the individual level. Until resolved, SAG 9 standards cannot be fully defined.

## SAG 10 — Resilience and Capacity Building

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Full title | Study and Evaluation Group No. 10 — Resilience and Capacity Building (R&CB) |
| SBTDWS role | SRO — validate delivery of vital capability development requirements |
| Current status | GAP (implied in Task 2 and broader directed tasks mapping) |
| Current constraint | No capability development methodology; no defined fielding standard |

SAG 10 requires SBTDWS to help validate that PG force elements are developing and fielding vital capabilities as directed. This maps closely to the experimentation and capability validation functions of the future ADWC.

---

## Why SAG 9 and SAG 10 Matter

These two SAGs define the **validation ceiling** for SBTDWS. They are the hardest and most politically sensitive outputs the school is being asked to deliver.

From the [[ADWC FIC Analysis]]:

- Validation is only meaningful if SBTDWS has **endorsed standards** — agreed criteria against which units are measured.
- SBTDWS needs **authority to assess, identify gaps, and recommend remediation** — not just to observe.
- Command must be willing to **protect honest reporting** — adverse assessments will face institutional resistance.
- The [[OC-T Cadre Development Plan]] sits entirely on this path: no trained OT cadre = no real SAG 9/10 validation.

---

## Validation Authority (unresolved)

> [!gap] Validation authority gap
> Validation is only meaningful if SBTDWS is given endorsed standards, a clear reporting pathway, and authority to identify gaps honestly. Without this, validation remains advisory and cannot support real FE@R (Force Elements at Readiness) decisions.

Until Higher HQ confirms: (a) the standards, (b) the reporting chain, and (c) whether SBTDWS can certify, fail, or only advise — SAG 9 and SAG 10 validation cannot be operationalised.

---

## Critical Path

SAG 9 / SAG 10 validation by 2030 requires, in sequence:

1. Higher HQ endorses SAG standards (who owns this?)
2. IW / UW distinction clarified (what are we validating?)
3. OT cadre trained and accredited (who validates? — see [[OC-T Cadre Development Plan]])
4. Validation authority confirmed (can we fail a unit?)
5. First validation event conducted (proof of concept)
6. Reporting pathway established (does the result mean anything?)

None of these have been completed. All are on the critical path to IOC by 2029.
