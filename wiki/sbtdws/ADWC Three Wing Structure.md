# ADWC Three Wing Structure

A proposed three-wing structure for ADWC, mapped from the Australian CTC-L / JTW framework. This is a cleaner structural model than the earlier gap-analysis framing — it names the wings, assigns their roles, and identifies the main effort explicitly.

Navigation: [[sbtdws/_index]] | [[CTC-L JTW Reference Model]] | [[ADWC Transformation Concept]]

---

## Overview

| Wing | Australian Analog | Role | Priority |
|------|-----------------|------|----------|
| Desert Warfare Wing (DWW) | JTW | Specialist individual and environmental training — feeder | Supporting |
| CTVMR Wing | CTC-L | Collective Training, Validation, and Mission Rehearsal — the readiness certifier | **Main Effort** |
| TEIW | No analog (novel) | Tactical Experimentation and Innovation | Supporting (narrow charter) |

---

## Wing 1 — Desert Warfare Wing (DWW)

**Australian analog:** JTW (Joint Training Wing)

**Role:** Delivers specialist individual and environmental training. Individual Skills Wing + relevant E&E Wing courses. The feeder into CTVMR.

**Courses and functions:**
- Desert driving, navigation, survival, SERE, camels, integration
- Individual skills pipeline — the foundation all collective training builds on
- Foreign forces training (marketable as specialist input, not as the main product)

**The identity challenge:** Unlike JTW in the Australian model, DWW is not geographically separate from ADWC — it is transitioning *within* the same enterprise. This is the cultural communication challenge. The message must be clear: DWW is not being demoted. Individual specialist training is the *input* to collective readiness, not the *output*. DWW's success is measured by how well CTVMR performs, not by DWW's own throughput.

**Enhancements (minimal workload, significant effect):**
Adding UxS awareness, signature management, drone threat recognition, and counter-detection to existing courses transforms them into multi-domain-ready individual training without creating new courses. Enhancement replaces lesson content, not course structure.

## Wing 2 — CTVMR Wing

**Australian analog:** CTC-L (Combined Training Centre - Land)

**Role:** Collective Training, Validation, and Mission Rehearsal. **This is the main effort.** The wing that determines whether ADWC actually certifies readiness or merely runs exercises.

### Three Non-Negotiable Pillars

All three are interdependent. A weakness in any one degrades the other two.

**1. OPFOR (Opposing Force)**
- Dedicated OPFOR capability — not borrowed from training units, not role-players with insufficient authority
- OPFOR must be credible, persistent, and resourced to win
- Without credible OPFOR, exercises confirm existing behaviour rather than exposing gaps
- OPFOR must include: FPV/UxS operators, EW capability, IW/deception actors, hybrid threat behaviours

**2. OC/T Cadre (Observer/Controller/Trainer)**
- The human element that converts exercise events into learning
- Must be trained, accredited, and empowered with authority to fail
- This is the single most important near-term investment decision (see Key Finding below)
- Links directly to: SAG 9/10 validation authority, E&E Wing retraining, OT cadre IOC target Q2 27

**3. AAR Process (After Action Review)**
- Structured, rigorous, non-punitive
- Converts observed behaviour into corrective insight
- Must be embedded in every collective serial — not optional
- AAR data must feed the TEIW (lessons to experimentation pipeline)

### Mission Rehearsal Function

ADWC's Mission Rehearsal function *exceeds* the Australian CTC-L model — it draws closer to the NTC/JRTC pre-deployment MRX construct. This is a deliberate design advantage and must be preserved as a distinct design requirement, not collapsed into general collective training.

**Mission Rehearsal requires:**
- EXCON and HICON capability
- Scenario design cell
- Hameem infrastructure (IOC Q4 28)
- Instrumentation and digital integration (J6 backbone dependency)

## Wing 3 — TEIW

**Australian analog:** None — genuinely novel. The Australians gesture toward this in CTC 2025 Concept but have not formalised it.

**Role:** Tactical Experimentation and Innovation Wing. Converts operational problems into tested TTPs and capability recommendations.

### Critical Constraint: Narrow Charter

The biggest risk to TEIW is **scope creep absorbing resources from CTVMR before the main effort is mature.**

The charter must be narrow and explicitly tied to:
- Supported operational requirements (not speculative technology exploration)
- FDC 5 governance under J3 WDC
- Makhlab 1 as the first bounded sprint
- Transition gate to PGIC/ATI-Hub once those structures are established

TEIW is not the ATI-Hub. It is the tactical execution node — the field-level experimentation function that feeds results upward to PGIC.

**TEIW functions:**
- Run governed experimentation sprints (Makhlab 1 model)
- Design and execute CAPDEV trials with hypotheses and measures of effectiveness
- Produce TTP and doctrine outputs
- Feed AAR data from CTVMR back into experiment design

## Key Finding

> **OC/T cadre development is the single most important near-term decision.**
>
> It has the longest lead time of any CTVMR pillar. It is the element that most determines whether CTVMR actually delivers learning or just delivers training activity. Without it, ADWC certifies participation, not readiness.

**Implication for timeline:** OT cadre must begin development in PREP phase to reach IOC by Q2 27. Every month of delay on this decision compounds directly into the MLOC gate.

## What This Model Changes vs Earlier Framing

The earlier [[ADWC Transformation Concept]] framing identified seven gaps and five ADWC functions. The three-wing model simplifies this into an executable structure:

- DWW = feeder (individual readiness)
- CTVMR = main effort (collective readiness, validation, MRX)
- TEIW = narrow innovation pipeline (doctrine output, FDC 5)

The five ADWC functions from the earlier analysis map cleanly onto this:

| ADWC Function | Wing |
|-------------|------|
| Collective Training | CTVMR |
| Validation | CTVMR (OC/T pillar) |
| Mission Rehearsal | CTVMR (MRX sub-function) |
| Tactical Experimentation | TEIW |
| Doctrine and Standards | SAT Cell / cross-cutting |
