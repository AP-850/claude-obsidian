# SBTDWS Barriers and Blockers

Institutional barriers identified that could impede SBTDWS transformation. These are real issues documented in a barriers paper reviewed and incorporated into the transformation analysis. Final products used deliberate command-safe language rather than stating the problems directly.

Navigation: [[sbtdws/_index]] | [[ADWC Transformation Concept]] | [[ADWC FIC Analysis]]

> [!gap] Sensitivity note
> These barriers are real and documented. The command briefs and transformation products used softer language (see below) to allow the message to be received without triggering defensive responses.

---

## The Barriers (Plain Language)

| Barrier | Impact |
|---------|--------|
| Inflated pass rates | Validation is advisory in practice; units learn they will pass |
| Lack of constructive feedback | Students do not learn from assessment; OTs do not challenge |
| Weak instructor accountability | Low standards reinforced over time; poor performers retained |
| Resistance to change at mid-level management | O5-level resistance is the named political risk for transformation |
| Overreliance on civilian contractors for fundamental skills | Military ownership of core competencies is eroding |
| Resource shortages | Training continuity disrupted; equipment unavailable |
| Bureaucratic systems causing delays or cancellations | Training schedules repeatedly disrupted by administrative overhead |
| Tendency to preserve appearance of success | Deficiencies are hidden rather than surfaced and fixed |

---

## Command-Safe Language Used in Briefs

The transformation products translated these barriers into language suitable for the command environment:

| Plain barrier | Command-safe framing |
|--------------|---------------------|
| Inflated pass rates | "Strengthen assessment credibility" |
| Lack of constructive feedback | "Improve visibility of training outcomes" |
| Weak instructor accountability | "Mature instructor standards" |
| Resistance to change | "Build institutional capacity" |
| Contractor dependency | "Consolidate military ownership of core skills" |
| Resource shortages | "Strengthen training system foundations" |
| Bureaucratic delays | "Improve training continuity" |
| Hiding deficiencies | "Enhance readiness assurance" |

---

## Implications for Transformation

These barriers do not prevent transformation — but they define the floor that must be raised before higher functions (validation, experimentation, mission rehearsal) can succeed.

The sequence matters:
1. Fix the foundations first — assessment credibility, instructor standards, feedback loops
2. Then build the higher functions — OT cadre, SAG 9/10 validation, EXCON, Hameem
3. Branding without fixing the floor creates reputational risk

> [!contradiction] Tension with transformation timeline
> The SBTDWS 2030 Timeline requires IOC (Q1 29) — approximately 3 years from now. The barriers listed above are cultural and institutional, which take longer to fix than capability or infrastructure gaps. There is a real risk the transformation timeline does not allow sufficient time for the cultural transformation to precede the capability build.

---

## Connection to Key Risks

- **O5 resistance** — named as the primary political risk at the structural reorganisation inflection point. Mid-level management who benefit from the current system will resist the transparency and accountability that transformation requires.
- **Validation authority** — without the institutional will to fail units, the OT cadre and EXCON architecture will not produce honest assessments.
- **Command protection** — the [[ADWC FIC Analysis]] notes that command must actively protect honest reporting for the validation system to work.
