# OPFOR and Red Force Concept

Concept for a dedicated Opposing Force (OPFOR) / Red Force capability for SBTDWS as a desert warfare training institute. OPFOR is the adversary element that enables realistic, stress-tested collective training.

Navigation: [[sbtdws/_index]] | [[SBTDWS CTC Architecture]] | [[Combat Training Centre Model]]

---

## Why OPFOR Matters

Training against a predictable or inert adversary produces forces that pass exercises, not forces that can fight. The NTC benchmark (see [[Combat Training Centre Model]]) is 11th ACR as dedicated OPFOR — approximately 4,000 soldiers whose sole mission is to be a realistic enemy.

OPFOR creates the conditions for:
- Tactical surprise and friction
- Command decisions under pressure
- Logistics stress
- Communication failure and recovery
- Honest assessment of unit performance

---

## Core OPFOR Elements for SBTDWS

### 1. Training Scenarios
Realistic, challenging desert warfare scenarios including:
- Reconnaissance and counter-reconnaissance
- Ambushes in terrain-complex environments
- Raids on positions and infrastructure
- Counter-insurgency and grey-zone operations
- Urban edge and compound clearance

Scenarios should be built from historical events, current conflict analysis, and threat intelligence (J2 input) — not from generic templates.

### 2. Equipment and Vehicles
OPFOR requires:
- High-mobility desert vehicles (different from Blue Force for visual identification)
- UAVs / FPV drones (adversary perspective)
- Communications equipment that mirrors realistic adversary C2
- Simulated weapons and pyrotechnics for realism

### 3. Role Players
Sources for OPFOR role players:
- Retired military personnel (desert warfare experience preferred)
- Active personnel in non-deployed rotations
- Trained civilian contractors with military background
- Partnered units designated as OPFOR on rotation

Role players must: be fully briefed on TTPs, maintain confidentiality, understand the training objectives, and be assessed after each serial.

### 4. Training Aids
- Pyrotechnics and simulated weapon effects
- MILES or equivalent instrumentation (hit / kill confirmation)
- Special effects (smoke, sound, night illumination)
- UAV feeds for OPFOR situational awareness

### 5. Simulation Integration
OPFOR should connect to the LVC (Live, Virtual, Constructive) architecture:
- OPFOR controllers can inject virtual threats (aircraft, distant artillery)
- Constructive forces extend the OPFOR mass beyond physical headcount
- AAR playback from instrumented OPFOR activity

### 6. Evaluation Role
OPFOR is not just an adversary — it is an evaluation tool. OPFOR debriefs should record:
- What worked for them (Blue Force vulnerabilities)
- What they expected vs what happened
- Tactical innovation by Blue Force worth capturing

## Current Status

OPFOR capability at SBTDWS is currently limited. The [[SBTDWS CTC Architecture]] identifies the OPFOR Cell as required for full CTC function. No dedicated OPFOR formation exists. The [[Combat Training Centre Model]] shows that NTC's dedicated 11th ACR is the gold standard — SBTDWS would need a much smaller but dedicated element.

---

## OPFOR Force Design Concept

A minimal SBTDWS OPFOR element would require:
- 1 × OPFOR OC (Captain/Major level)
- 3-4 × OPFOR section commanders (trained, experienced)
- 10-15 × OPFOR soldiers per rotation
- Supplemented by role players for scenario complexity
- Instrumentation support from J7 / EXCON

A rotating OPFOR model (units fill OPFOR on rotation) is a viable interim option but produces variable quality. A permanent cadre is the long-term solution.
