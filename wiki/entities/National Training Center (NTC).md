# National Training Center (NTC)

US Army Combat Training Center at Fort Irwin, California. The closest global analogue to ADWC's operational environment and the gold standard for instrumented desert collective training and certification.

Navigation: [[Research: Global ADWC Equivalents]] | [[Combat Training Centre Model]]

---

## Overview

Fort Irwin sits in the Mojave Desert. The NTC provides a realistic instrumented battlefield for brigade-level combined arms force-on-force training and mission rehearsal. Approximately 10 rotations per year, 28 days each, 3,000–5,000 soldiers per rotation.

The desert environment is the training feature — heat, dust, long sight lines, sparse cover, and austere logistics are not obstacles to training. They ARE the training.

---

## Structure

**OPFOR — 11th Armored Cavalry Regiment (Blackhorse)**
- Permanently stationed at NTC; primary mission is OPFOR
- Hand-picked soldiers: infantry, tankers, field artillerymen
- Full-time, persistent, resourced to win
- Reactivated 26 October 1994 specifically for the NTC OPFOR role
- Now incorporating sUAS and emerging technology skills

**OC/T Teams (Operations Group)**
- Organized by battle function: Scorpions, Dragons, and others
- One OC/T team per unit echelon (platoon through brigade)
- 600+ AARs per rotation
- Phase 1 certification: two-day classroom training during RSOI before the rotation begins

**Assessment Output**
- Structured AARs at every echelon
- Take Home Package — formal assessment of unit performance across all warfighting functions
- Adjutant General review: determines whether training requirements have been met

---

## Training Capability

- Instrumented battlefield: MILES (laser direct fire simulation), RTLS (Real-Time Location System, GPS-based), JANUS battle staff simulation for indirect fire
- Live fire and force-on-force combined
- Pre-deployment Mission Rehearsal Exercise (MRE): tailored to the unit's specific future mission set — distinct from a standard decisive action rotation
- 180-day planning timeline recommended for rotational units

---

## Desert Specificity — What Makes NTC Irreplaceable

| Environmental factor | Training effect |
|---------------------|----------------|
| Extreme heat | Forces genuine load-out and sustainment decisions; dehydration and equipment failure cannot be simulated |
| Long sight lines | Demands ISR discipline and signature management — cannot hide like temperate woodland |
| Sparse cover | Forces use of terrain, deception, and movement technique |
| Austere logistics | True sustainment planning required; no easy resupply |
| Dust and sand | Mechanical and equipment reliability challenges; maintenance culture tested |

No other training environment produces these simultaneously. This is why NTC exists in the Mojave, not in a temperate forest.

---

## What ADWC Can Learn

1. **Dedicated, persistent OPFOR is non-negotiable.** The 11th ACR exists only for this mission. Borrowed OPFOR or role-players with insufficient authority consistently produce weaker training outcomes.
2. **600+ AARs per rotation.** Volume and rigour of structured feedback is what makes the NTC model work — not the terrain alone.
3. **OC/T certification matters.** Two-day classroom certification before the rotation; OC/Ts know the doctrine they are assessing.
4. **MRE as a distinct product.** The NTC separates standard decisive action rotations from pre-deployment MREs tailored to the unit's specific mission. ADWC's Mission Rehearsal function should make the same distinction explicitly.
5. **Desert environment as the product.** NTC's terrain is not a backdrop. It is a core training element. ADWC should frame its desert environment the same way — not "we train in the desert" but "the desert is what we train FOR."

---

## Where ADWC Exceeds NTC

- NTC serves US forces and some allies. ADWC serves PG and regional partners in THEIR own operational theatre — strategic authenticity NTC cannot provide.
- NTC's UxS integration is emerging. ADWC's CAPDEV portfolio (FPV, paramotor, UGV, drone resupply) and TEIW positions it to develop desert UxS TTPs from first principles.
- NTC does not do UW/SERE validation (SAG 9 equivalent). ADWC does.
