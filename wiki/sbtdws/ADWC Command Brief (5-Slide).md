# ADWC Command Brief — 5 Slides

**Audience:** O6 CoC at SBTDWS  
**Purpose:** Secure endorsement of the three-wing design, the sequencing, and three specific command decisions  
**Format:** B&W where possible. Thematic only for the three-wing diagram (Slide 3). No jargon without definition. One idea per slide.  
**Tone:** Honest assessment. No optimistic briefing. The O6 CoC trusts candour more than confidence.

Navigation: [[sbtdws/_index]] | [[ADWC Design Synthesis]] | [[OC-T Cadre Development Plan]]

---

## Slide 1 — Context

> **HEADLINE:**
> SBTDWS is becoming the Presidential Guard's desert readiness certifier — not a bigger training school.

**Body (on slide):**

The mission changes.

| Today | 2030 |
|-------|------|
| Train individual and collective skills | Certify units ready to fight |
| Measure throughput (courses completed) | Measure outcomes (formations validated) |
| Desert Warfare School | All Domain Warfare Centre |

The difference is not branding. It is what the institution produces.

**Visual note:** Simple two-column table. No graphics. Clean.

**Speaker note:**
"This brief is not about renaming SBTDWS. It is about changing what SBTDWS produces. Today we produce trained soldiers. By 2030 we produce a verdict: this formation is ready to prosecute All Domain Warfare in desert conditions — or it is not. That is a fundamentally different institution. This brief explains how we get there and what we need from this command."

---

## Slide 2 — The Design Principle

> **HEADLINE:**
> We are fixing foundations first. The brand follows the credibility — not the other way around.

**Body (on slide):**

Two principles govern every decision in this plan:

**Credibility before ambition.**
Fix the foundations — Observer Trainer cadre, validation authority, exercise standards — before declaring an All Domain Warfare Centre.

**Truth before excellence.**
An honest assessment of what SBTDWS cannot yet do is more valuable than an optimistic one. The nine directed tasks show 5 gaps. We name them. We sequence the fixes.

What this means in practice:
- No ADWC rebrand until the first formation is certified
- No Hameem capital spend until the validation function works on existing ground
- No experimentation wing expansion until the collective training wing is the main effort

**Visual note:** Clean text slide. Two bold principle headings in black. Bullets below each.

**Speaker note:**
"Every CTC that has failed — and there are several in this region — has made the same mistake: built the brand before the capability. We have been directed to become an ADWC. We will get there. But we will get there by being honest about what we are today and sequencing the work correctly. What you see on the next slide is the result of that sequencing."

## Slide 3 — The Three Wings

> **HEADLINE:**
> Three wings. One purpose: declare PG units ready — or not ready.

**Body (on slide — visual diagram):**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                             │
│   DESERT WARFARE WING          CTVMR WING                  │
│   ──────────────────           ──────────────────           │
│   Individual training     ──►  Collective Training          │
│   Environmental skills         Validation                   │
│   Desert pipeline              Mission Rehearsal            │
│                                                             │
│   FEEDS →                      ◄── MAIN EFFORT             │
│                                                             │
│                           ▲                                 │
│   TEIW                    │ doctrine                        │
│   ──────────────────       │                                │
│   Tactical Experimentation │                                │
│   FDC 5 / Makhlab 1        │                                │
│   Narrow charter           │                                │
│                                                             │
│              VERDICT: READY / NOT READY                     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Three points:

1. **DWW feeds CTVMR.** Individual training is the input to collective readiness, not the output. DWW's success is measured by CTVMR performance.

2. **CTVMR is the main effort.** The certification function. Three non-negotiables: dedicated OPFOR, Observer Trainer cadre, rigorous AAR.

3. **TEIW is deliberately narrow.** Tied to FDC 5 and J3 WDC only. It informs CTVMR. It does not compete with it for resources.

**Visual note:** Use a clean three-box diagram with directional arrows. CTVMR box slightly larger/bolder. "MAIN EFFORT" label under CTVMR. Colour: CTVMR in a dark fill/white text; others in outline only.

**Speaker note:**
"This is the design in one slide. The Desert Warfare Wing continues what it does today — individual desert training. That is the foundation. The new main effort is the CTVMR Wing: Collective Training, Validation, and Mission Rehearsal. That is the function SBTDWS has never had before — the authority to say: this brigade is ready, or it is not. The TEIW — Tactical Experimentation and Innovation — is deliberately kept narrow. It runs experiments tied to J3 WDC governance. It does not grow until CTVMR is working. The risk we are managing is scope creep — putting resources into the innovation function before the validation function exists."

## Slide 4 — The Sequence

> **HEADLINE:**
> Four actions. In this order. The first two start this month.

**Body (on slide):**

```
NOW ──────────────────────────────────────────────── 2030

  1           2           3           4
  │           │           │           │
  ▼           ▼           ▼           ▼
Start       Sign        Announce    Name the
OC/T        Force Gen   three-wing  first
cadre       Pipeline    structure   validated
pipeline    ToR         (Q4 26)     formation
(NOW)       (Q2 26)
```

**Action 1 — OC/T cadre development. Start now.**
13-month pipeline to IOC. No slack. The single longest-lead-time item in the design. Every month of delay pushes the Q2 27 validation IOC.

**Action 2 — Force Generation Pipeline ToR. Sign by Q2 26.**
Defines the SBTDWS/J7 lane split. Unlocks the J7 relationship and the validation authority pipeline. Due this quarter.

**Action 3 — Structural reorganisation. Q4 26.**
Announce the three wings after the 6GW training review is complete. Not before. The review tells us what to restructure.

**Action 4 — Name the first validated formation.**
The moment ADWC becomes real. Not a date — an event. Plan for it in 2027.

**Visual note:** Simple left-to-right timeline with four nodes. Actions 1 and 2 highlighted (they start now). Clean, no Gantt complexity.

**Speaker note:**
"The sequencing is the strategy. Action 1 and Action 2 can both start this month — neither requires capital, neither requires a structural change. Action 1 is the OC/T cadre pipeline, which I will brief separately. Action 2 is the Force Generation Pipeline ToR, which this command must drive with J3 and J7. Action 3 — announcing the three wings — cannot happen until the training review is done. We will not announce a structure and then change it. Action 4 is the most important milestone in this entire plan: naming a formation that has been assessed and declared ready. That is when the institution changes. Not before."

## Slide 5 — The Ask

> **HEADLINE:**
> Three command decisions unlock the first year.

**Body (on slide):**

| Decision | What it enables | When needed |
|----------|----------------|------------|
| **1. Endorse the three-wing design.** | Gives the Commandant authority to plan the restructure and communicate it to the force | Now |
| **2. Direct the Force Generation Pipeline ToR engagement with J3 and J7.** | Formalises the SBTDWS validation lane; unlocks J7 exercise programme access | Q2 26 |
| **3. Commit to backing the first adverse assessment.** | The OT cadre's authority to fail is only real if the O6 CoC is visibly behind it when a unit pushes back | Before Q2 27 |

**Bottom line:**

ADWC is achievable. The foundations exist. The design is sequenced correctly. The first two actions cost nothing and start now.

The single risk to the design is speed: **every month the OC/T cadre pipeline is delayed is a month off the Q2 27 IOC gate.**

This command's most important decision is not approving the design. It is approving the start date.

**Visual note:** Three-row table, clean. Decision 3 row slightly shaded to emphasise it is the hardest ask. Bottom line in bold italic text below table.

**Speaker note:**
"Decision 1 is conceptual endorsement. Decision 2 is direction to engage externally. Decision 3 is the hardest one and the most important: committing to stand behind an adverse assessment when a unit commander challenges it. An OT cadre without command backing is an audience. We are asking this command to make the certification real — which means accepting that some units will be told they are not ready, and supporting that verdict. If this command can commit to those three decisions, we can begin the OC/T pipeline this week."

## Brief Conduct Notes

**Length:** 20–25 minutes. 5 minutes per slide. Leave 10 minutes for questions.

**Handouts:** One-page summary (Slide 3 diagram + Slide 5 table). No full deck printed — the briefer carries the narrative.

**Questions to anticipate:**

*"What happens to the E&E Wing staff?"*
They become the OT cadre. This is a role elevation, not a redundancy. Their FST evaluator skills are the foundation of the new function.

*"Can we do this without Hameem?"*
Yes — IOC is achievable on existing SBTDWS ground. Company-level validation does not require Hameem infrastructure. Hameem enables formation-level MRX at Phase 2.

*"What is the cost?"*
Phase 1 (PREP through MLOC) is primarily staff effort and command engagement, not capital. The 80-item laundry list shows 21 of 38 P1 items are documents to write, decisions to confirm, or relationships to formalise. Capital comes at Phase 2.

*"What if the O5 staff resist the reorganisation?"*
This is the named risk. The mitigation is sequencing: announce the structure after the training review, not before. And make the first adverse assessment visibly consequential — O5 resistance collapses once the certification is real.
