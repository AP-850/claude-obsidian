# Valley of Death — Defense Innovation

The valley of death is the gap between technology prototype and program of record where promising capabilities die. It is the central failure mode in defense innovation and the primary problem a tool like TACTIX exists to solve.

Navigation: [[Research: Defense Innovation Governance Tools]] | [[Capability Gap Experimentation Platform]]

---

## Definition

A technology or capability that has been prototyped or demonstrated but fails to transition into a formal program of record or operational use. The capability exists; it simply cannot cross the institutional gap between demonstration and fielding.

---

## Why It Exists

Three concurrent barriers must be managed simultaneously (the "trifecta"):
1. Requirements — formal documentation needed before purchase
2. Acquisition — complex, unfamiliar procurement processes
3. Budget cycles — "colors of money", fiscal year timing

Managing these sequentially (the default) causes startup/prototype runway to expire before government processes align.

Secondary causes (Atlantic Council, 2023):
- JCIDS locks down requirements up front rather than enabling iterative adaptation
- Budget reprogramming requires prior approval rather than notification
- No single authority coordinates R&D, acquisition, and industry engagement
- Bridge funding absent: demonstrated capabilities sit idle for 2-4 years before reaching warfighters

---

## What Bridges It

Governance mechanisms that have worked (Source: [[atlantic-council-defense-innovation-adoption-2023]]):
- Portfolio-based (not program-based) acquisition with consolidated budgets
- Bridge funding to accelerate from demonstration to production
- Rapid requirements validation replacing JCIDS
- "Big three" selection panels (PM + innovation unit + warfighting unit) — ensures scalability is evaluated at demonstration, not after

Army PIT target: 90 days from selection to combat-ready solution (Source: [[army-pathway-for-innovation]]).
Historical baseline: 2-4 years from demonstration to warfighter. (Source: [[atlantic-council-defense-innovation-adoption-2023]])

---

## Relevance to TACTIX

The valley of death is the core problem TACTIX addresses. Stage 7 (Terminate/Improve/Scale) is explicitly the bridge — it generates the command decision brief that moves a prototype into a program of record or terminates it with documented rationale.

Without TACTIX (or equivalent), CAPDEV units manage this with Word documents and email, meaning:
- No audit trail for the decision
- No structured evidence base for the command brief
- No cross-experiment lessons capture
- High probability the capability dies in the valley

TACTIX's commercial value proposition: it is the governance tool that prevents capable innovations from dying between demonstration and fielding at the battalion/brigade level.
