# Task Force 59

The US Navy's first unmanned systems task force, established September 2021 in the 5th Fleet Area of Operations (Arabian Gulf). The most cited example of successful unit-level defense innovation governance.

Navigation: [[Research: Defense Innovation Governance Tools]]

---

## What It Is

A purpose-built task force to rapidly integrate unmanned systems and AI with maritime operations. Reached full operational capability in January 2023. Created a "digital ocean" of connected unmanned assets in the Arabian Gulf.

---

## Governance Model (Why It Works)

Three features distinguish TF 59 from standard military innovation efforts:

1. **Problem-centric structure** — organised around actual operational challenges, not bureaucratic requirements. The unit shape follows the problem, not the org chart.

2. **Meritocracy of ideas** — "the best ideas prevailed regardless of the rank of those who proposed them." Startup culture, not hierarchy. Junior operators could table solutions that senior officers adopted.

3. **Team of teams** — wide network across government, academia, and industry. Not a closed procurement process.

---

## Results

- Pioneered manned-unmanned fleet teaming in contested environments
- Integrated over 100 unmanned systems across the 5th Fleet AOR
- Demonstrated that rapid capability integration at unit level is achievable with the right governance model
- Influenced broader Navy and Allied unmanned systems programs

---

## Relevance to TACTIX and SBTDWS

TF 59 is the closest real-world analog to what Makhlab 1 and the TEIW are trying to build at SBTDWS. The governance principles directly apply:
- TEIW should adopt problem-centric structure (not Wing-hierarchy-centric)
- Makhlab 1 sprint should run as a meritocracy of ideas between Falcons Bde operators and SBTDWS staff
- TACTIX should reflect the TF 59 philosophy in its UI: flat, problem-first, output-oriented

The Atlantic Council Commission cited TF 59 as the best governance model for unit-level defense innovation (Source: [[atlantic-council-defense-innovation-adoption-2023]]).
