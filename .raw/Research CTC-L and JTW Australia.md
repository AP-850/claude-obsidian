## Analysis Summary

### Core Structural Mapping

The Australian CTC solved almost exactly the same problem ADWC faces — an enterprise whose centre of gravity had been a specialised environmental wing (JTW) that needed to be repositioned as a feeder while a collective training/certification capability became the main effort. That transition is the roadmap.

### Three Proposed ADWC Wings

**Desert Warfare Wing (DWW)** — Direct JTW analog with one important distinction: JTW never had to manage its own identity subordination because it was always geographically and institutionally separate from CTC-L. DWW is transitioning *within* the same enterprise, so the cultural communication challenge is harder.

**CTVMR Wing** — The CTC-L analog and the main effort. Three pillars — OPFOR, OC/T cadre, and AAR process — are non-negotiable and interdependent. The Mission Rehearsal function is where ADWC actually *exceeds* the Australian model, drawing it closer to the NTC/JRTC pre-deployment MRX construct. That's worth preserving as a distinct design requirement.

**TEIW** — Genuinely novel. The Australians gesture toward this in their CTC 2025 Concept but haven't formalised it as a wing. The biggest risk here is scope creep absorbing resources from CTVMR before the main effort is mature. The analysis recommends a narrow charter tied explicitly to supported operational requirements.

### Key Finding

The single most important near-term decision: **OC/T cadre development** — it has the longest lead time and is the element that most determines whether CTVMR actually delivers learning or just delivers training activity.
