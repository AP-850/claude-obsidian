# Unconventional Warfare and Resilience

Doctrinal framework covering Unconventional Warfare (UW) and Resilience and Capacity Building (R&CB) — the two SOF skillsets that underpin SAG 9 and SAG 10, SBTDWS's two highest-priority validation responsibilities by 2030.

## Unconventional Warfare (UW)

### Definition
UW is military and paramilitary operations conducted in enemy-held, enemy-controlled, or politically sensitive territory. It includes the organisation, training, equipment, and direction of indigenous forces. Primary reference: FM 3-05.130 (Army Special Forces UW) and ATP 3-05.1.

### Core activities
- Guerrilla warfare
- Subversion
- Sabotage
- Intelligence collection
- Escape and evasion
- Support to resistance movements

### Key doctrinal distinction
UW is distinct from Irregular Warfare (IW) more broadly. IW encompasses a range of activities (counterinsurgency, foreign internal defense, stability operations, etc.). UW specifically involves working with, through, or by proxy forces in denied or contested environments.

### Relevance to SBTDWS
SAG 9 is titled "Combined Unconventional Warfare (Joint)" — meaning SBTDWS must be able to validate PG force elements' ability to conduct UW in joint and combined contexts. This is significantly more demanding than individual IW course delivery.

## Resilience and Capacity Building (R&CB)

### Definition
R&CB encompasses Security Force Assistance (SFA), Foreign Internal Defense (FID), and resistance support. It involves building the capacity of partner forces, civil institutions, and societies to resist threats and sustain themselves through crisis.

### NATO baseline requirements (Article 3 framework)
NATO's resilience concept identifies seven baseline requirements:
1. Continuity of government and critical services
2. Resilient energy supply
3. Ability to cope with mass casualties
4. Resilient food and water supply
5. Resilient communications systems
6. Resilient civil transport
7. Resilient civil-military cooperation

### For SBTDWS / SAG 10
SAG 10 (Resilience and Capacity Building) requires SBTDWS to validate PG force elements' ability to contribute to these functions. This connects to:
- SERE-B / personnel recovery (see ADF Personnel Recovery Framework)
- IW training pipelines (PGI / IWTC)
- Force generation for partnered operations
- Supporting host-nation capacity development in the region

## IW vs UW — The Unresolved Distinction

> [!gap] Higher HQ direction required
> The key unresolved question: what is the difference between the Pl(-) IW capability being grown across all PG units (an individual training product) and the UW capability being handed off from SOC (a force generation product)? These are different things requiring different training architectures and different accountability chains. SBTDWS validation scope depends on this distinction being clarified.

| Dimension | IW (Pl-) capability | UW from SOC |
|-----------|--------------------|-----------| 
| Who develops it | PGI / IWTC (individual courses) | SOC (force generation, handoff) |
| Where it sits | Across all PG units at Pl(-) level | Specialist sub-units |
| What SBTDWS validates | Collective IW employment | Combined UW operations |
| What SAG it maps to | Partially SAG 9 | SAG 9 (primary) |

Until this is resolved, SBTDWS cannot define its OT standards for SAG 9 validation.
