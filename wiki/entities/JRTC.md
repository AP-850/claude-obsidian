# JRTC — Joint Readiness Training Center

US Army Combat Training Center at Fort Johnson (formerly Fort Polk), Louisiana. Counterpart to NTC — focuses on light infantry, SOF, airborne, and joint operations in subtropical terrain. Explicitly capable of both Decisive Action and Mission Rehearsal Exercise (MRE) environments.

Navigation: [[Research: Global ADWC Equivalents]] | [[Combat Training Centre Model]] | [[National Training Center (NTC)]]

---

## Overview

JRTC complements NTC: where NTC is heavy combined arms in desert terrain, JRTC is light infantry, SOF, and joint operations in the Louisiana piney woods and swamp. Both use the same core model (OPFOR + OC/T + AAR) but are optimised for different force types and missions.

---

## Structure

**OPFOR**
- Dedicated opposing force
- Replicates complex threat environments including hybrid and irregular adversaries
- Designed to challenge light infantry, airborne, and SOF formations

**OC/T System**
- One OCT per platoon and company — matched by Military Occupational Specialty (MOS) to the unit being assessed
- Duty: observe unit performance, control engagements, teach doctrine, coach to improve, monitor safety, conduct professional AARs
- Intervene only for safety issues or doctrine violations — otherwise observe without interference
- OC/T Academy trains all incoming observer coaches before they begin duty

**Multi-Domain Integration**
- US Air Force integrated into exercises (CAS, ISR, EW)
- Host nation and civilian role players simulate diverse operational environments
- Interagency integration routinely practised

---

## Decisive Action vs MRE

JRTC explicitly offers two rotation types:

| Type | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Decisive Action (DA) | High-intensity combined arms training — assessing unit warfighting capability |
| Mission Rehearsal Exercise (MRE) | Pre-deployment rehearsal — tailored to the specific future mission the unit will execute |

This distinction is directly relevant to ADWC's Mission Rehearsal sub-function design. MRE is not a lighter rotation — it is a different product with a different purpose.

---

## What ADWC Can Learn

1. **MOS-matched OC/Ts.** Assigning an OC/T with the same specialty as the unit being assessed produces higher-quality coaching and more credible authority to fail.
2. **MRE as a distinct, separately-designed product.** ADWC should explicitly offer both validation rotations (assessing collective readiness) and MREs (rehearsing a specific upcoming operation).
3. **Interagency integration.** JRTC routinely includes non-military actors. ADWC's hybrid threat and IW/UW scenarios would benefit from the same.
4. **Light/SOF-specific OC/T training.** The JRTC Academy model — certifying OC/Ts before they begin duty — is directly applicable to ADWC's OT cadre development plan.
