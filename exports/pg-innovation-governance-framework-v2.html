PG Innovation Governance

A framework for where innovation lives — owner and participant agnostic

This is a starting framework, not a proposal to hand any single organisation the answer. It maps how an idea should move from an individual or unit through to a fielded, institutionalised capability, and separates "who leads innovation" into the questions it actually contains. Revised after review — see the opening move below.

Suggested opening move

Everyone in the room will want to own the idea. Almost no one will want to own the work — and whoever does grab a piece is likely to run it alone, without inviting the others in. Worth saying explicitly before any option below gets discussed. The single best defence against it: open with roles, not with options.

The Flow — Idea to Capability

7 stages
01 Idea Source Any operator, any unit — not one pilot brigade
→
02 Intake & Sponsorship No mechanism exists today, for anyone
→
03 Tier 1 Experiment Bounded, existing resources, 30–90 days
04 Tier 2 Gate Capital / scale decision — governance matters here
→
05 Scale & Field Fielding dossier, small-run production, handover
→
06 Standardise Cross-unit, once more than one runs it
→
07 Institutionalise Doctrine, training pipeline, force design review

The gap that matters most is Stage 2, not Stage 4. The room will likely default to arguing about who chairs the Tier 2 gate — but nobody currently owns getting an idea from an individual to a governed experiment in the first place, regardless of who wins that argument.

"Who Leads" Is Three Questions

not one
1

Portfolio Governance

Priorities, resourcing, the Tier 2 kill/continue call, cross-unit deconfliction. This is the actual "who leads" question.

2

Execution

Running the sprints. Stays distributed at unit/school level — doesn't need a single owner.

3

Sponsorship

Naming problems, providing operators, validating outputs. Inherently unit-level, not a headquarters function.

Governance-Chair Options

for stage 4 only
Option A

Existing Capability-Development Function Leads

StrengthAlready does this work today. No new stand-up.
RiskInherits a process-first, compliance culture — the documented cause of capability death in the gap between prototype and program. May also lack the institutional authority to make a ring-fenced cell stick.
MitigationRing-fence a small, flat cell with delegated authority and real institutional standing, explicitly separate from the procurement chain.
Option B

Warfighting Staff (J3) Leads

StrengthAlready the de facto owner on paper. Fewest new coordination seams.
RiskConcentration — already carries readiness, targeting, C2. Scouting is a different discipline from staff planning.
MitigationKeep mandate-setting authority; delegate day-to-day portfolio management to a distinct small cell.
Option C

New Entity Leads

StrengthMatches the pattern other institutions use — a distinct engine, not bolted onto an existing function.
RiskSlowest to stand up — charter, staffing, facility, competing for the same institutional bandwidth as other pending decisions.
MitigationStand up an interim governance board now; let the formal charter run in parallel, not gate everything until it lands.
Synthesis

Standing Board, Not a Single Department

A chair for mandate-setting, a scouting/capability-development function held distinct from procurement, and rotating unit representation for demand signal and execution — rather than one department owning all three questions above. Best supported by outside comparative practice; not a foregone conclusion.
