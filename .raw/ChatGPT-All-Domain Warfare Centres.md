## Examples of existing All-Domain / Multi-Domain centres (or very similar)

### 1) **National All-Domain Warfighting Center (NADWC) - Michigan National Guard (USA)**
A purpose-built “all-domain” training construct that ties together a major training area (Camp Grayling) with an innovation node at an air base (Selfridge ANGB). It’s explicitly framed as an “All-Domain Warfighting Center” offering training to wider DoD entities. ([Army](https://www.army.mil/article/240736/michigan_national_guard_goes_all_in_on_all_domain_training?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Why it matters:** this is one of the cleanest, most literal examples of what you’re asking for: *a branded, standing “all-domain warfighting center”* rather than a concept.

---

### 2) **NATO Joint Warfare Centre (JWC) - Stavanger (Norway)**
NATO’s JWC is a standing joint warfare and training institution that designs and runs high-end exercises with a **“360-degree multi-domain setting”** and is chartered to produce synthetic scenarios. ([NATO Joint Warfare Centre](https://www.jwc.nato.int/our-work/scenario-development/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))  
They also explicitly position themselves as delivering **multi-domain exercises** and driving warfare development from concept into execution. ([NATO Joint Warfare Centre](https://www.jwc.nato.int/article/jwc-launches-campaign-plan/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Why it matters:** If you want a model for a *joint / coalition* all-domain centre that blends **scenario design + experimentation + lessons capture**, JWC is a mature reference point.

---

### 3) **US Air Force Warfare Center (USAFWC) - Nellis AFB (USA)**
USAFWC positions its focus as **“All-Domain Air Force Warfighters”** and sits above major operational test/training infrastructure (e.g., Red Flag ecosystem). ([Nellis Air Force Base](https://www.nellis.af.mil/About/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/284150/us-air-force-warfare-center/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Why it matters:** It’s a “warfare center” construct that’s **training + tactics + test infrastructure**, even if the service is air-focused.

---

### 4) **US Air Force 505th Command and Control Wing (505 CCW) - operational-level C2 across domains**
The 505 CCW is the USAF’s only wing dedicated to operational-level C2, specializing in C2 in **air, space, and cyberspace** and translating testing/tactics/training into operational C2 practice. ([505th Combat Communications Wing](https://www.505ccw.acc.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/376114/505th-command-and-control-wing/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))  
(They are also tied to the USAF Warfare Center construct via reporting relationships.) ([U.S. Department of War](https://www.war.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3494747/505th-ccws-change-of-command-season-concludes/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Why it matters:** This is the “C2 integration engine” model - less about ranges and more about **how decisions, effects, and information move across domains**.

---

### 5) **US Army Multi-Domain Task Force (MDTF) - not a centre, but the “integration nucleus”**
MDTFs are described by CRS as the Army’s **“organizational centerpiece”** for multi-domain operations. ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11797?utm_source=chatgpt.com))  
In exercises, you see “hub” constructs emerge (e.g., Land Effects Coordination Center serving as an integrating C2 structure). ([PACOM](https://www.pacom.mil/Media/NEWS/News-Article-View/Article/4268012/3d-multi-domain-task-force-achieves-historic-effects-and-interoperability-first/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Why it matters:** If your aim is an “all-domain warfare centre” that actually *delivers effects*, the MDTF pattern shows what the operational integration element looks like (as opposed to an academic/training centre).

## How these centres are typically set up (the common architecture)

Across the examples above, you’ll usually find **one of three archetypes** - many organisations combine two.

### Archetype A: **Training / exercise / scenario centre**
**Purpose:** rehearse multi-domain operations, validate plans, build headquarters teams, generate lessons learned.  
**Looks like:** NATO JWC; parts of USAFWC; NADWC. ([NATO Joint Warfare Centre](https://www.jwc.nato.int/our-work/scenario-development/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Typical setup**
- **Command group:** Director/Commandant + small joint staff
- **Scenario & wargaming division:** scenario writers, red team/OPFOR, scripting, sim integration
- **Exercise division:** event design, control (EXCON), white cell, assessments
- **Lessons / doctrine link:** captures TTPs and feeds doctrine/training pipelines
- **Tech backbone:** sims, networks, instrumentation, data capture

---

### Archetype B: **Operational integration / effects coordination “hub”**
**Purpose:** synchronize sensors-to-shooters-to-effects across domains (real ops or high-fidelity exercises).  
**Looks like:** MDTF-style coordination hubs (e.g., LECC) and 505 CCW’s operational-C2 focus. ([PACOM](https://www.pacom.mil/Media/NEWS/News-Article-View/Article/4268012/3d-multi-domain-task-force-achieves-historic-effects-and-interoperability-first/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Typical setup**
- **Ops integration cell (core):** joint fires, EW, cyber, space, ISR fusion, information ops, targeting
- **Authorities & liaison model:** embedded reps from each domain owner to reduce “permission latency”
- **Battle rhythm:** daily effects boards, targeting cycles, collection management sync, legal/policy checks
- **Data / comms:** common operational picture + data standards + cross-domain release processes

---

### Archetype C: **Experimentation / rapid capability development centre**
**Purpose:** connect industry/tech to operators; run trials; “system of systems” integration events.  
**Looks like:** NIWC-style integration experiments; parts of NADWC’s innovation node. ([U.S. Navy](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/display-news/Article/3724548/niwc-atlantic-conducts-largest-ever-all-domain-naval-integration-event/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Typical setup**
- **Experimentation office:** test design, hypotheses, measures of performance/effectiveness
- **Engineering & integration:** software, networks, data, interoperability, digital twins
- **Operational user group:** end-users define problems and iterate quickly
- **Governance:** pathways to procurement / fielding / doctrine change

---

## The “minimum viable” org design (what you’d stand up first)

If you were building an “All-Domain Warfare Centre” in a modern force, a proven starting point is:

1) **Directorate + Governance**
- Director, Deputy, Chief of Staff
- Steering board with domain owners (land/air/maritime/cyber/space/info) and acquisition/tech lead

2) **3 Core Divisions**
- **Exercise & Scenario Division** (build reps + certify HQs) - JWC pattern ([NATO Joint Warfare Centre](https://www.jwc.nato.int/our-work/scenario-development/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))  
- **Integration & C2 Division** (effects coordination hub) - 505/MDTF pattern ([505th Combat Communications Wing](https://www.505ccw.acc.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/376114/505th-command-and-control-wing/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))  
- **Experimentation & Prototyping Division** (trial + integrate) - NIWC pattern ([U.S. Navy](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/display-news/Article/3724548/niwc-atlantic-conducts-largest-ever-all-domain-naval-integration-event/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))  

3) **Federated “Nodes,” not one monolith**
- A physical training/range node (where you can instrument and run reps)
- A C2 / digital node (where you can run integration, data, sims, AAR tooling)

That “two-node” approach is exactly what makes NADWC-like constructs practical: *training space + innovation/C2 space* under one brand. ([Army](https://www.army.mil/article/240736/michigan_national_guard_goes_all_in_on_all_domain_training?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This concept proposes the development of:

**Saeed Bin Tahnoun All-Domain Warfare Centre (SBT-ADWC)**

- Desert warfare training

- Capability development
- Tactical innovation
- Operational integration
