# JMRC — Joint Multinational Readiness Center

US Army Europe's Combat Training Center, located at Hohenfels Training Area, Bavaria, Germany. The "NTC of Europe" — same CTC model applied to a multinational brigade in temperate European terrain.

Navigation: [[Research: Global ADWC Equivalents]] | [[Combat Training Centre Model]]

---

## Overview

JMRC is the fourth US Army CTC (alongside NTC, JRTC, and Mission Command Training Program). It trains leaders, staffs, and units up to Brigade Combat Team (+) level, with a specific focus on multinational combined arms operations.

---

## Distinctive Features

**Multinational format:** A US BCT headquarters leads a brigade containing one organic US maneuver battalion and two attached multinational partner battalions. This is coalition training by design — not an add-on.

**Deployable instrumentation:** Unlike NTC (fixed Mojave infrastructure), JMRC operates an expeditionary instrumentation system. OC/T teams and OPFOR packages can deploy beyond the Hohenfels box, extending the training environment to geographically separated units.

**OPFOR:** Capabilities-based, persistent, realistic. JMRC OPFOR recently added small UAS (sUAS) skills as part of a dedicated Drone Validation Course — reflecting the current operational threat.

**OC/T Academy:** Trains incoming OC/Ts before they begin duty. Provides quality coaching, teaching, and mentoring with formal and informal AAR capability.

---

## What ADWC Can Learn

1. **Coalition training as a core design feature.** ADWC's foreign forces training and partner force integration should be structured like JMRC — not as a special event but as a routine format where partner battalions train alongside PG units under PG validation standards.
2. **Deployable instrumentation.** If ADWC cannot instrument every training area, deployable OC/T packages that extend beyond the main site (Hameem) allow validation reach without permanent infrastructure at every location.
3. **OPFOR must evolve.** JMRC added drone skills to its OPFOR as the threat evolved. ADWC's OPFOR must integrate FPV, EW, and hybrid threat behaviours from the outset — not retrofitted later.
